CSF Analysis: Pattern Recognition in Meningitis and Neurology

Interpret CSF cells, glucose, protein and opening pressure together, not as isolated diagnostic cutoffs.

Quick Revision

  • Bacterial meningitis often causes neutrophil-predominant inflammation and lower CSF glucose, although early patterns can vary.
  • Viral meningitis more often has a lymphocyte-predominant pattern with relatively preserved glucose.
  • CSF results depend on sampling time, prior treatment, blood contamination, immune status and age.
01

Start with the patient

  • Evaluate contraindications and procedural safety before lumbar puncture; never delay urgent care for a critically unwell patient.
  • Compare CSF glucose with a near-contemporaneous blood glucose rather than treating the absolute number alone as definitive.
02

Recognize common patterns

  • Bacterial, viral, tuberculous and fungal infections have overlapping but useful patterns in cell differential, protein and glucose.
  • Albuminocytological dissociation is associated with Guillain–Barré syndrome but may be absent early.
03

Examination pitfalls

  • Normal or nearly normal early CSF cannot fully exclude dangerous infection.
  • Microbiologic methods and overall clinical suspicion may matter more than a memorized fixed cell-count cutoff.
