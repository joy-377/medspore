Glaucoma: Primary Open-Angle vs Acute Angle-Closure

Distinguish slowly progressive open-angle glaucoma from acute symptomatic angle closure, an ocular emergency.

Quick Revision

  • Glaucoma causes progressive optic nerve damage; intraocular pressure is an important risk factor but a single pressure value is not the diagnosis.
  • Open-angle disease often progresses without pain or obvious early vision symptoms.
  • Acute angle closure may present with severe ocular pain, red eye, halos, nausea and rapid visual deterioration.
01

Recognize the two major presentations

  • Open-angle glaucoma can produce optic disc cupping and characteristic visual-field defects.
  • Acute angle closure may produce corneal haze, a mid-dilated pupil and markedly elevated intraocular pressure, warranting emergency ophthalmic assessment.
02

Assessment and treatment principles

  • Evaluate intraocular pressure, optic nerve, visual field and angle anatomy as indicated.
  • Angle closure requires urgent eye-pressure reduction and definitive angle management by an ophthalmologist.
03

Exam trap

  • Normal-range measured pressure does not rule out every form of glaucoma.
  • Do not delay treatment of acute painful vision loss while waiting for outpatient investigations.
