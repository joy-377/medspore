Acute Severe Asthma and COPD Exacerbations: Bedside Priorities

Recognize respiratory failure early; assess work of breathing and mental status rather than relying only on wheeze.

Quick Revision

  • Severe asthma can present with minimal audible wheeze when airflow is critically restricted.
  • Initial treatment pathways include oxygen titration, rapid-acting inhaled bronchodilator therapy and systemic anti-inflammatory treatment when indicated.
  • COPD exacerbations require attention to hypercapnic risk, infection or cardiac triggers and possible ventilatory support.
01

Find danger signs

  • Exhaustion, altered consciousness, inability to speak, reduced air movement or worsening hypoxemia require escalation.
  • Assess peak flow when appropriate without delaying treatment of an unstable patient.
02

Manage by physiology

  • Use acute-asthma or COPD pathways matched to severity, gases, oxygenation and response.
  • Monitor for rising carbon dioxide or fatigue; noninvasive or invasive ventilation decisions are individualized.
03

Exam trap

  • A quiet chest can be ominous, not reassuring.
  • Routine unmonitored high-flow oxygen is not appropriate for every COPD exacerbation.
