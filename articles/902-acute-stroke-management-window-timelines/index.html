Acute Ischemic Stroke: Recognition, Imaging and Reperfusion Decisions

Stroke treatment depends on last-known-well time, neurological disability, vessel status and imaging—not one memorized number.

Quick Revision

  • Recognize sudden focal deficits; assess airway, glucose, neurological findings and last-known-well time.
  • Noncontrast brain imaging is used urgently to assess for hemorrhage and guide reperfusion decisions.
  • Eligibility for thrombolysis or thrombectomy depends on clinical and imaging selection under current pathways.
01

First actions

  • Activate a stroke system of care and assess whether symptoms are disabling.
  • Do not dismiss posterior circulation symptoms such as ataxia or diplopia simply because classic face-arm-speech findings are absent.
02

Use the right treatment pathway

  • Intravenous thrombolysis is time-dependent in eligible patients; selected extended-window and mechanical thrombectomy strategies use additional clinical and imaging criteria.
  • Blood pressure and antithrombotic decisions depend on treatment pathway and contraindications.
03

Exam trap

  • Do not assume a normal early CT rules out ischemic stroke.
  • Do not substitute antiplatelet treatment for eligible reperfusion therapy.

For a complementary clinical comparison, read TIA Recognition and Secondary Stroke Prevention Principles.
