01

1. Epidural Hematoma (EDH)

1. Epidural Hematoma (EDH):
• Vessel Ruptured: Middle Meningeal Artery (often associated with temporal bone fracture).
• Clinical Hallmarks: Classic 'Lucid Interval' where the patient is initially conscious after trauma, followed by rapid neurological deterioration as the hematoma expands.
• CT Head Appearance: Biconvex, Lens-shaped hyperdensity that is sharply bounded by cranial suture lines (because periosteum is tightly adherent to sutures).

02

2. Subdural Hematoma (SDH)

2. Subdural Hematoma (SDH):
• Vessel Ruptured: Bridging Veins (tears due to deceleration or rotational shear forces; common in elderly and alcoholics with cortical atrophy).
• CT Head Appearance: Crescent-shaped, Concave hyperdensity (or hypodense/isodense if chronic) that can cross cranial suture lines.
• Associated Sign: Often accompanied by extensive underlying parenchymal contusion and a significant Midline Shift across the falx cerebri, causing uncal herniation and compression of CN III (blown pupil).
