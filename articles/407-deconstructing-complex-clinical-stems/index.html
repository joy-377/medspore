01

1. Read the Last Sentence First

1. Read the Last Sentence First:
Read the final interrogation line before reading the 10-line patient history. This clarifies whether the question asks for a diagnosis, next best step, mechanism of action, or contraindication, preventing redundant re-reading.

02

2. Identify Age, Gender, and Chronology Anchors

2. Identify Age, Gender, and Chronology Anchors:
• Young female + joint pain + rash = Think SLE.
• Elderly male + weight loss + painless jaundice = Think Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma.
• Child + periorbital edema + cola-colored urine = Think Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN).

03

3. Avoid the 'Anchor Trap'

3. Avoid the 'Anchor Trap':
Do not lock into a diagnosis based on a single early symptom if later vital signs and physical findings clearly contradict it.
