Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

When a patient presents to the Emergency Room, ruling out life-threatening conditions is priority number one. Never ignore these classic red flags:

  • Thunderclap Headache: Reaches maximum intensity within one minute. Diagnosis to rule out: Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH). Next step: Non-contrast CT Head. If CT is negative but suspicion remains high, perform a Lumbar Puncture to look for xanthochromia.
  • Tearing Chest Pain Radiating to the Back: Diagnosis to rule out: Aortic Dissection. Next step: Check for asymmetric pulse and blood pressure between arms. Order CT Angiography or Transesophageal Echocardiography (TEE).
  • Pain Out of Proportion to Examination: Classic for Acute Mesenteric Ischemia or Necrotizing Fasciitis.
  • Sudden Painless Vision Loss: Think Central Retinal Artery Occlusion (CRAO). A true ophthalmologic emergency.

Takeaway: In emergency medicine, it is better to over-investigate a red flag than to miss a lethal diagnosis.
