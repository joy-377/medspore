Trauma & Orthopedic Triads

Unhappy triad of the knee, Fat Embolism syndrome, and Lethal Triad of Trauma...

Triads & Signs · Surgical Triads

  • O'Donoghue's Unhappy Triad (Knee Injury): 1. ACL tear, 2. MCL tear, 3. Medial Meniscus tear (though lateral meniscus is often more commonly involved in acute settings). Caused by lateral blow to a planted leg.
  • Bergman's Triad (Fat Embolism Syndrome): 1. Dyspnea/Hypoxia, 2. Neurological abnormalities (confusion), 3. Petechial rash (chest/axilla/conjunctiva). Usually occurs 24-72 hours after a long bone (femur) fracture.
  • Lethal Triad of Trauma: 1. Hypothermia, 2. Coagulopathy, 3. Metabolic Acidosis. Management target: Damage control surgery to stop the bleeding, rewarming, and massive transfusion protocol.
