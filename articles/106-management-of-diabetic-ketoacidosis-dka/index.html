Management of Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)

The IV fluids, Insulin infusion, and Potassium correction protocol...

Clinical Tips · Ward Protocols

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

DKA is characterized by Hyperglycemia, Ketosis, and Metabolic Acidosis. Management must follow a strict sequence to prevent cerebral edema and hypokalemia.

  • 1. IV Fluids (The most critical first step): Start with 0.9% Normal Saline (NS) at 15-20 mL/kg/hr for the first hour to restore intravascular volume. Once blood glucose falls to 200 mg/dL, switch to 5% Dextrose with 0.45% NS.
  • 2. Insulin Therapy: Give a 0.1 U/kg IV bolus of Regular Insulin, followed by a continuous infusion of 0.1 U/kg/hr. Never stop insulin until the anion gap closes (acidosis resolves), even if glucose drops.
  • 3. Potassium Correction: Total body potassium is always depleted in DKA, even if serum levels appear falsely high initially. If serum K+ is < 3.3 mEq/L, hold insulin and give IV KCl first. If K+ is between 3.3 and 5.2 mEq/L, add KCl to the IV fluids.
  • 4. Bicarbonate: Only indicated if the pH is severely low (< 6.9).
