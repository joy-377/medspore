Hyponatremia: Osmolality, Symptoms and Safe Correction

A stepwise approach separates true hypotonic hyponatremia from translocational or analytical explanations.

Quick Revision

  • First evaluate neurological symptoms and measured serum osmolality; do not classify hyponatremia from the sodium number alone.
  • After confirming hypotonic hyponatremia, assess volume status, medication causes and urine osmolality/sodium.
  • Overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia can cause osmotic demyelination.
01

Classify by effective osmolality

  • Hyperglycemia can lower measured sodium through water shifts; this differs from true hypotonic water excess.
  • Normal measured osmolality in an unexpected low sodium result raises the possibility of a measurement-related problem in selected settings.
02

Identify the physiology

  • Volume status, renal concentrating response, diuretics, endocrine disease and fluid intake guide the differential.
  • Management is tailored to symptoms, chronicity, clinical status and measured repeat values.
03

Exam trap — correction is not a race

  • Severe neurological symptoms require emergency supervised management; mild longstanding cases need a different pathway.
  • Correction can accelerate unexpectedly after the cause resolves, making frequent monitoring essential.
