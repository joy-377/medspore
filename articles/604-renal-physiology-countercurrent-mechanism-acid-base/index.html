Renal Physiology: Countercurrent Mechanism & Acid-Base

The nephron concentrates urine through loop countercurrent multiplication, vascular exchange and hormone-regulated water permeability.

Quick Revision

  • The descending thin limb is relatively water-permeable, while the thick ascending limb reabsorbs salts and is water-impermeable.
  • The medullary osmotic gradient facilitates water reabsorption in the collecting system when antidiuretic hormone acts.
  • Urea recycling contributes to the inner medullary osmotic gradient.
01

Countercurrent multiplier

  • Different permeabilities in descending and ascending nephron segments create the medullary gradient.
  • The thick ascending limb reabsorbs NaCl without accompanying water, diluting tubular fluid.
02

Exchange and hormonal control

  • Vasa recta preserve the gradient through countercurrent exchange while minimizing solute washout.
  • ADH increases collecting duct water permeability through aquaporin regulation.
03

Exam trap

  • Loop of Henle segments are not uniformly water-permeable.
  • Concentrating ability depends on both the gradient and the collecting duct response to ADH.
