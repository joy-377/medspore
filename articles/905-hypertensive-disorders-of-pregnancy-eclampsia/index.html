Preeclampsia and Eclampsia: Clinical Recognition and Escalation

High blood pressure after mid-pregnancy plus organ injury can signal preeclampsia even without overt proteinuria.

Quick Revision

  • Preeclampsia may involve hypertension with kidney, liver, hematologic, pulmonary or neurologic consequences.
  • Eclampsia is new-onset seizure associated with the hypertensive pregnancy syndrome after other causes are considered.
  • Magnesium sulfate is the established anticonvulsant used in eclampsia pathways; obstetric escalation and delivery planning are essential.
01

Recognize maternal and fetal risk

  • Severe headache, visual symptoms, breathlessness, severe hypertension, upper abdominal pain and seizures warrant urgent assessment.
  • Evaluate fetal status, maternal laboratory tests and gestation while stabilizing the mother.
02

Seizure protection and care

  • Use a protocol-approved magnesium sulfate regimen with monitoring for respiratory compromise, reflexes and urinary function.
  • Severe hypertension management, seizure control and the timing of delivery require specialist obstetric coordination.
03

Exam trap

  • Proteinuria is not required when other qualifying organ dysfunction is present.
  • Do not regard absence of edema as evidence that preeclampsia cannot be severe.
