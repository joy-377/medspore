Malignant Hyperthermia: Recognize the Anesthetic Emergency

Malignant hyperthermia is a hypermetabolic anesthetic crisis; rapid recognition and removal of triggering agents are vital.

Quick Revision

  • Triggering volatile anesthetics and succinylcholine can provoke malignant hyperthermia in susceptible patients.
  • Early clues include rising end-tidal carbon dioxide, tachycardia, rigidity and metabolic or respiratory acidosis.
  • Stop triggers, call for help, give dantrolene and provide intensive physiological support using a rehearsed protocol.
01

Mechanism and recognition

  • Dysregulated skeletal-muscle calcium release drives hypermetabolism, heat production and muscle breakdown.
  • Hyperthermia may develop after earlier physiologic abnormalities; waiting for a high temperature may delay recognition.
02

Emergency management

  • Stop triggering agents, ventilate with oxygen, administer dantrolene according to the current crisis protocol and correct complications.
  • Monitor electrolytes, rhythm, kidney function, temperature and recurrence risk.
03

Exam trap

  • Dantrolene is a specific therapy; naloxone is not the reversal agent for malignant hyperthermia.
  • Early ETCO2 rise may be more informative than a late fever spike.
