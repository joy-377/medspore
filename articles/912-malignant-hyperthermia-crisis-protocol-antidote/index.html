Malignant Hyperthermia: Crisis Protocol & Antidote

RYR1 gene mutation, Succinylcholine trigger, and IV Dantrolene reconstitution...

Final Year (Part 2) · Anesthesia

Pathophysiology:
Autosomal dominant pharmacogenetic disorder of skeletal muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release channel (RYR1 gene in >70% of cases, or CACNA1S). Exposure to triggering agents causes uncontrolled, massive myoplasmic calcium release leading to hypermetabolism, sustained muscle rigidity, and rhabdomyolysis.

Triggering Agents:
• All Halogenated Inhalational Anesthetics (Halothane, Isoflurane, Sevoflurane, Desflurane).
• Depolarizing muscle relaxant: Succinylcholine.
• Safe Drugs: Propofol, Etomidate, Ketamine, Opioids, Non-depolarizing muscle relaxants (Vecuronium, Rocuronium), and Local anesthetics.

Earliest Clinical Markers:
1. Unexplained, steep rise in End-Tidal CO2 (EtCO2) refractory to increased minute ventilation (earliest and most reliable sign).
2. Masseter muscle spasm / rigidity (Trismus) following succinylcholine administration.
3. Sinus tachycardia, tachypnea, unstable BP.
• Hyperthermia (>40°C): Highly specific, but typically a late sign (can rise by 1°C every 5 minutes).

Emergency Management Protocol:
• 1. Discontinue Trigger: Stop inhalational agent immediately; hyperventilate with 100% Oxygen at high flow (≥10 L/min).
• 2. Specific Antidote: Administer IV Dantrolene immediately. Initial dose is 2.5 mg/kg IV push, repeated every 5–10 minutes until symptoms subside (up to a maximum dose of 10 mg/kg). (Dantrolene binds RYR1 channels and blocks calcium egress).
• 3. Active Cooling: Cold IV normal saline infusions, surface ice packs over groin, axilla, and neck.
• 4. Treat Hyperkalemia & Acidosis: IV Calcium gluconate, Insulin + Dextrose, and Sodium bicarbonate.
