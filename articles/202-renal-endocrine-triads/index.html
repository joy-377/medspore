Renal & Endocrine Triads

Four classic renal/endocrine patterns—HUS, Whipple triad, pheochromocytoma and mixed cryoglobulinemia—are most useful when connected to mechanisms.

Quick Revision

  • Hemolytic uremic syndrome classically combines microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia and acute kidney injury.
  • Whipple triad requires compatible symptoms, measured low glucose at the time of symptoms and relief when glucose is corrected.
  • Pheochromocytoma can cause episodic headache, sweating, palpitations and hypertension.
01

HUS: hematology meets the kidney

  • Small-vessel injury produces red-cell fragmentation, thrombocytopenia and kidney dysfunction.
  • A diarrheal prodrome can occur in Shiga-toxin-associated HUS but is not present in every thrombotic microangiopathy.
02

Whipple triad: confirm true hypoglycemia

  • Document compatible symptoms, low circulating glucose during symptoms, and improvement after glucose correction.
  • The triad characterizes genuine symptomatic hypoglycemia; it does not establish insulinoma as the cause by itself.
03

Pheochromocytoma and cryoglobulinemia

  • Episodic headache + sweating + palpitations is the classic pheochromocytoma memory pattern.
  • Meltzer triad in mixed cryoglobulinemia is palpable purpura, arthralgia and weakness; other causes can produce similar features.
04

Exam trap

  • Do not diagnose a specific endocrine tumor solely from a triad.
  • An apparently characteristic presentation still requires appropriate biochemical or hematologic confirmation.
