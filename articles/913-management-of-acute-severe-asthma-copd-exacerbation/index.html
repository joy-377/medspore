Management of Acute Severe Asthma & COPD Exacerbation

Nebulized bronchodilators, systemic steroids, IV magnesium sulfate, and NIV criteria...

Final Year (Part 2) · Medicine

Acute Severe Asthma / COPD Exacerbation Management:
• 1. Oxygen Therapy: Target saturation 94–98% for asthma; 88–92% for COPD (to prevent suppression of hypoxic respiratory drive leading to CO2 narcosis).
• 2. Bronchodilators: Frequent or continuous nebulization of short-acting beta-2 agonists (Salbutamol) combined with short-acting muscarinic antagonists (Ipratropium bromide).
• 3. Systemic Corticosteroids: IV Hydrocortisone (100 mg every 6h) or Oral Prednisolone (40-50 mg daily) must be given early (takes 4-6 hours to kick in).
• 4. Adjunctive Therapies: IV Magnesium Sulfate (2g over 20 min) in severe acute asthma refractory to initial treatment.
• 5. Non-Invasive Ventilation (NIV / BiPAP): Indicated in acute COPD exacerbations with persistent respiratory acidosis (pH < 7.35 and pCO2 > 45 mmHg). Endotracheal intubation is the final resort if NIV fails.
