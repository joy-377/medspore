Cell Injury & Patterns of Tissue Necrosis

The pattern of necrosis reflects mechanism and tissue context, providing strong pathology examination clues.

Quick Revision

  • Coagulative necrosis commonly follows ischemia in solid organs outside the central nervous system.
  • Liquefactive necrosis is characteristic of cerebral infarction and many suppurative infections.
  • Caseous, fat and fibrinoid necrosis each have distinct inflammatory or metabolic associations.
01

Coagulative versus liquefactive

  • Coagulative: tissue outlines persist transiently after ischemic injury in many solid organs.
  • Liquefactive: enzymatic digestion dominates, as in brain infarction and abscess formation.
02

Caseous and fat necrosis

  • Caseous: amorphous granular debris in granulomatous inflammation, classically associated with tuberculosis but not unique to it.
  • Fat necrosis: lipase-related destruction in pancreatitis or trauma can cause calcium soap formation (saponification).
03

Fibrinoid vascular injury

  • Fibrinoid change in vessel walls reflects marked vascular injury with plasma-protein/fibrin-related deposition and can occur in some immune-mediated vasculitides.
04

Exam trap

  • Do not assume every infarct is coagulative: brain ischemia is the classic exception.
