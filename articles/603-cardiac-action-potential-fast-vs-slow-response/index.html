Cardiac Action Potential: Fast vs Slow Response

Ventricular myocyte phases vs SA nodal automaticity and ion channels...

First Year MBBS · Physiology

Ventricular Myocyte Action Potential (Fast Response - 5 Phases):
• Phase 0 (Rapid Depolarization): Rapid Na+ influx through voltage-gated fast Na+ channels (Inactivated by Tetrodotoxin).
• Phase 1 (Early Partial Repolarization): Inactivation of Na+ channels, transient outward K+ current (Ito).
• Phase 2 (Plateau): Balance between inward Ca2+ current through L-type Ca2+ channels and outward delayed rectifier K+ current (IK). Crucial for excitation-contraction coupling and long refractory period.
• Phase 3 (Rapid Repolarization): Closure of Ca2+ channels, massive K+ efflux through delayed rectifier channels.
• Phase 4 (Resting Membrane Potential): Stable at around -90 mV maintained by inward rectifier K+ channels (IK1) and Na+/K+ ATPase.

SA Node Action Potential (Slow Response / Pacemaker - 3 Phases):
• Phase 4 (Pacemaker Potential / Prepotential): Spontaneous prepotential driven by Funny current (If) — mixed Na+/K+ influx activated by hyperpolarization, followed by transient T-type Ca2+ channels.
• Phase 0 (Depolarization): Driven purely by Ca2+ influx via L-type channels (NO fast Na+ channels involved).
• Phase 3 (Repolarization): Ca2+ channel closure, K+ efflux. Resting potential unstable (-60 mV).
