Medical & Surgical Stratification: CURB-65 & Child-Pugh

Apply CURB-65 to pneumonia severity assessment alongside clinical judgement; understand the purpose of Child-Pugh and Apgar scoring.

Quick Revision

  • CURB-65 summarizes confusion, urea, respiratory rate, blood pressure and age in adults with community-acquired pneumonia.
  • Child-Pugh describes chronic liver-disease severity using bilirubin, albumin, coagulation, ascites and encephalopathy.
  • Apgar summarizes neonatal condition after birth; resuscitation decisions are not based on waiting for the Apgar score.
01

Pneumonia: CURB-65

  • The five components assess new confusion, elevated urea, tachypnea, hypotension and older age; each adds one point.
  • Use the score together with oxygenation, comorbidities, pregnancy and clinical judgement when deciding place of care.
02

Liver disease and newborn assessment

  • Child-Pugh classes use bilirubin, albumin, INR/prothrombin-time measures, ascites and encephalopathy; it helps describe prognostic severity.
  • Apgar evaluates appearance, pulse, grimace response, activity and respiration at defined early postnatal time points.
03

Memory aid

  • CURB-65: Confusion, Urea, Respiratory rate, Blood pressure, Age 65 or more.
  • The score supports clinical judgment, not a rigid discharge rule.
04

Exam trap

  • A scoring tool assists but never overrides dangerous clinical findings.
  • Apgar is not a reason to delay effective newborn ventilation.
