Acute Pancreatitis: Diagnosis, Severity and Supportive Care

Diagnose pancreatitis using compatible pain, pancreatic enzymes and/or imaging; management depends on severity and cause.

Quick Revision

  • The diagnosis often relies on a typical clinical syndrome and elevated pancreatic enzymes, with imaging when needed.
  • Gallstones and alcohol are common causes; other etiologies include drugs and metabolic disorders.
  • Early treatment emphasizes analgesia, appropriate fluid assessment, nutrition and recognition of organ dysfunction.
01

Clinical pattern

  • Upper abdominal pain may radiate to the back, with nausea or vomiting.
  • Lipase is generally preferred over amylase in the diagnostic workup.
02

Severity and complications

  • Evaluate organ dysfunction, hemodynamic status, respiratory function and complications rather than relying only on an older scoring system.
  • Address gallstone-related obstruction or infected complications according to clinical findings and specialist pathways.
03

Exam trap

  • A high enzyme concentration alone does not grade disease severity.
  • Prophylactic antibiotics are not routine for uncomplicated sterile pancreatitis.
