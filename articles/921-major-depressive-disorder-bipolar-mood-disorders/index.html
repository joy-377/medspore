Major Depressive Disorder & Bipolar Mood Disorders

DSM-5 criteria, melancholic features, lithium toxicity, and mood stabilizers...

Final Year (Part 2) · Psychiatry

Major Depressive Episode (MDE) Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5):
Must have ≥5 of the following symptoms present during the same 2-week period representing a change from previous functioning, with at least symptom #1 or #2 mandatory:
1. Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day.
2. Diminished interest or pleasure in all or almost all activities (Anhedonia).
3. Significant weight loss or gain (>5% in a month) or appetite changes.
4. Insomnia or hypersomnia nearly every day.
5. Psychomotor agitation or retardation nearly every day.
6. Fatigue or loss of energy nearly every day.
7. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive inappropriate guilt.
8. Diminished ability to think/concentrate or indecisiveness.
9. Recurrent thoughts of death, suicidal ideation, or suicide attempt.

Bipolar Disorder & Lithium Management:
• Bipolar I: Requires at least one Manic Episode (elevated/irritable mood for ≥1 week with grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, flight of ideas, risky behavior).
• Lithium Therapy: First-line mood stabilizer for Bipolar disorder and suicide prevention. Narrow therapeutic window (0.6–1.2 mEq/L).
• Lithium Toxicity Signs: Tremors, ataxia, dysarthria, confusion, coarse tremors, seizures. Precipitated by dehydration, sodium depletion, NSAIDs, and thiazide diuretics.
