Renal Physiology: Countercurrent Mechanism & Acid-Base

Medullary hyperosmolarity, loop diuretics site, and bicarbonate reclamation...

First Year MBBS · Physiology

Countercurrent Multiplier & Exchanger:
• Countercurrent Multiplier: Handled by the Loop of Henle. Descending thin limb is permeable to water but impermeable to NaCl (tubular fluid concentrates up to 1200 mOsm/L). Thick ascending limb actively pumps NaCl into interstitium via Na+-K+-2Cl- symporter (NKCC2) but is impermeable to water (diluting segment).
• Countercurrent Exchanger: Passive process in the Vasa Recta that maintains hyperosmolar medullary gradient without washing it out.
• Urea Recycling: Inner medullary collecting ducts reabsorb urea via UT-A1/UT-A3 under ADH influence, contributing 50% to medullary hypertonicity.

Tubular Bicarbonate Handling:
• Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT): Reabsorbs 85–90% of filtered HCO3- via apical Na+/H+ exchanger (NHE3) and membrane-bound Carbonic Anhydrase type IV (inhibited by Acetazolamide).
• Distal Tubule (Alpha-Intercalated Cells): Secretes H+ ions into lumen via H+-ATPase and H+/K+-ATPase, generating new HCO3-.
