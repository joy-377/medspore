Glomerular Diseases: Nephritic vs Nephrotic Syndromes

MCD, FSGS, Membranous, PSGN, and RPGN immunofluorescence patterns...

Second Year MBBS · Pathology

Nephrotic Syndrome (Heavy Proteinuria > 3.5 g/day, Hypoalbuminemia, Edema, Hyperlipidemia):
• Minimal Change Disease (MCD): Most common in children. LM normal; IF negative; EM shows effacement of visceral epithelial foot processes (podocytes). Selective proteinuria. Responds dramatically to corticosteroids.
• Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS): Most common nephrotic in adults (especially African Americans, HIV, heroin abuse). Segmental sclerosis; non-responsive to steroids.
• Membranous Nephropathy: Associated with PLA2R antibodies, Hep B, NSAIDs, solid malignancies. Diffuse capillary wall thickening, 'Spike and Dome' appearance on silver stain; Granular IgG/C3 along GBM.

Nephritic Syndrome (Hematuria, RBC casts, Oliguria, Hypertension, Mild Proteinuria):
• Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (PSGN): Follows group A strep skin (impetigo) or pharyngeal infection after 1–4 weeks. Low C3. 'Starry-sky' / lumpy-bumpy granular IF; Subepithelial electron-dense humps on EM.
• Rapidly Progressive Glomerulonephritis (RPGN): Severe nephritic picture progressing to renal failure within weeks. Crescent formation on LM (crescents made of parietal epithelial cells and monocytes/fibrin).
