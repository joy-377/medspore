Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding: Stabilization Before Endoscopy

Risk assessment, hemodynamic stabilization and early endoscopic evaluation are the practical core of UGIB care.

Quick Revision

  • Hematemesis and melena may reflect upper GI bleeding; hemodynamic instability takes priority over identifying the precise lesion.
  • Large-bore access, blood counts, crossmatch and a transfusion strategy require assessment of the patient and comorbidities.
  • Endoscopy provides diagnosis and frequently treatment after initial stabilization.
01

Recognize urgency

  • Repeated syncope, shock, ongoing hematemesis and altered mentation signal higher risk.
  • Consider variceal bleeding in the setting of portal hypertension and chronic liver disease.
02

Treat the cause

  • Peptic ulcer bleeding, varices, erosions and Mallory–Weiss tears require different targeted treatment plans.
  • Resuscitation, appropriate medication and early specialist coordination are complementary to endoscopy.
03

Exam trap

  • A near-normal initial hemoglobin does not exclude acute major hemorrhage.
  • Do not assume every black stool represents an active upper GI bleed without clinical assessment.

What is an immediate priority in severe upper GI bleeding?
