Normal Labor Stages & Partograph Interpretation

Assess labor through maternal and fetal wellbeing, cervical change and the overall pattern of progress rather than a rigid clock alone.

Quick Revision

  • Normal labor has variable progress; a single hourly cervical-dilation rule should not replace clinical assessment.
  • Evaluate contraction quality, fetal presentation, maternal physiology and fetal heart rate patterns.
  • The WHO Labour Care Guide promotes individualized, evidence-based intrapartum monitoring.
01

Observe progress in context

  • Document cervical findings, contractions, descent and maternal/fetal condition with an appropriate care tool.
  • Active labor and the pace of change vary substantially between individuals.
02

When progress appears slow

  • Review maternal and fetal status and consider presentation, position, contractions and support before selecting intervention.
  • Escalation decisions are based on a combination of findings and established maternity protocols.
03

Exam trap

  • A single slow cervical examination is not proof of obstructed labor.
  • Do not apply an outdated universal dilation threshold without context.

What is assessed using a labor progress chart?

For medical learning and exam revision. Clinical decisions require individualized assessment and current local protocols.
