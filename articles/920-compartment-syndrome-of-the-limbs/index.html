Acute Compartment Syndrome: The Time-Critical Limb

Rising pressure within a noncompliant compartment can compromise perfusion and nerves before pulses disappear.

Quick Revision

  • Pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch are classic early warning features.
  • Sensory disturbance and tense compartments increase concern; pulses may remain present until late.
  • Urgent surgical assessment and removal of external constriction are crucial when suspected.
01

Recognition

  • Trauma, fractures, reperfusion and tight casts or dressings are common contexts.
  • Serial examination matters when symptoms evolve after an initially reassuring assessment.
02

Physiologic logic

  • Pressure reduces local capillary perfusion and threatens ischemic muscle and nerves.
  • Objective pressure measurements may assist in selected difficult assessments, but do not replace clinical urgency.
03

Exam trap

  • Palpable distal pulses do not exclude compartment syndrome.
  • Do not wait for paralysis or pulselessness in a patient with strong early clinical warning signs.
