Incisional Hernia vs Intestinal Strangulation

Final Year (Part 2) · Surgery

Special Hernia Variants:
• Richter's Hernia: Only a portion of the circumference of the bowel wall is entrapped in the hernial ring. Danger: Strangulation and gangrene can occur without causing clinical intestinal obstruction (no vomiting or constipation, making diagnosis deceivingly difficult).

• Littre's Hernia: Hernial sac contains a Meckel's Diverticulum.

• Spigelian Hernia: Herniation through the Spigelian fascia (semilunar line), usually below the level of the umbilicus.

• Obturator Hernia: Herniation through the obturator canal; seen most commonly in elderly, thin, multiparous women. Presents with Howship-Romberg sign (pain down the medial aspect of the thigh to the knee due to obturator nerve compression).

Surgical Management: Open or laparoscopic mesh repair (e.g., Lichtenstein repair for inguinal hernia, sublay mesh repair for incisional hernia) to tension-free reinforce the abdominal wall defect.
