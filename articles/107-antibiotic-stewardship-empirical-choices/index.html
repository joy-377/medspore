Antibiotic Stewardship: Empirical Choices

What to prescribe for CAP, UTI, Meningitis, and Cellulitis before cultures arrive...

Clinical Tips · Prescription Pearls

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Choosing the right empirical antibiotic saves lives and prevents resistance. Here are the standard first-line empirical choices (always adjust based on local antibiogram and culture results later):

  • Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) - Outpatient: Amoxicillin (high-dose) OR Macrolide (Azithromycin) if atypical coverage is needed.
  • CAP - Inpatient (Non-ICU): Respiratory Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin) OR Beta-lactam (Ceftriaxone) + Macrolide.
  • Uncomplicated UTI (Cystitis): Nitrofurantoin (for 5 days) OR Fosfomycin (single dose) OR TMP-SMX (if resistance is low). Avoid Ciprofloxacin as first-line to save fluoroquinolones.
  • Acute Bacterial Meningitis (Adults): Ceftriaxone (covers N. meningitidis and S. pneumoniae) + Vancomycin (covers resistant pneumococcus). Add Ampicillin if patient is >50 years old or immunocompromised (to cover Listeria).
  • Purulent Cellulitis / Abscess: Must cover MRSA. Prescribe Oral TMP-SMX, Doxycycline, or Clindamycin. (For non-purulent cellulitis, Cephalexin is preferred to cover Strep).
