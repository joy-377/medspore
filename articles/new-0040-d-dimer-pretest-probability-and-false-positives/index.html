  • High clinical probability for pulmonary embolism generally requires appropriate imaging or another definitive approach, not D-dimer reassurance.

01

Interpret the result in context

  • D-dimer is sensitive to fibrin turnover but nonspecific; infection, pregnancy, surgery and age can increase it.
  • Its diagnostic value depends on pretest probability and use of a validated rule-out strategy.
02

Clinical interpretation & next steps

  • High clinical probability for pulmonary embolism generally requires appropriate imaging or another definitive approach, not D-dimer reassurance.
  • Different laboratories and assays use different units and cutoffs, so interpret the report accordingly.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • A positive D-dimer cannot confirm pulmonary embolism by itself.
