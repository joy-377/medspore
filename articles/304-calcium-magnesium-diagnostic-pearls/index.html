Calcium & Magnesium Diagnostic Pearls

Interpret calcium and magnesium disorders using symptoms, ionized calcium and a careful review of albumin and medications.

Quick Revision

  • When calcium results conflict with the clinical picture, check ionized calcium rather than relying only on albumin-adjusted totals.
  • Symptomatic hypocalcemia may cause perioral tingling, tetany, seizures and QT prolongation; hypercalcemia may cause dehydration and altered cognition.
  • Hypomagnesemia can impair parathyroid hormone action and make associated potassium or calcium abnormalities refractory.
01

Check what the calcium test actually measures

  • Total calcium is affected by albumin, while ionized calcium reflects the biologically active fraction.
  • Albumin-corrected calcium formulas are imperfect, particularly in critical illness, and should not replace ionized measurement when decisions are consequential.
02

Recognize the clinical contrasts

  • Hypocalcemia can cause neuromuscular excitability, muscle spasms and prolonged QT interval.
  • Hypercalcemia can cause polyuria, thirst, constipation, confusion and a shortened QT interval.
03

Choose cause-directed assessment

  • Review renal function, phosphate, magnesium, parathyroid hormone and vitamin D in the appropriate clinical context.
  • Severe symptomatic abnormalities require an urgent, monitored treatment pathway.
04

Exam trap: magnesium can be the missing link

  • Persistent hypocalcemia or hypokalemia despite replacement may reflect hypomagnesemia.
  • A single total calcium number without albumin and ionized context can be misleading.
