Trauma & Orthopedic Triads

Trauma patterns combine orthopedic injury, fat embolism and the physiologic lethal triad of major hemorrhage.

Quick Revision

  • The classical unhappy triad describes ACL, MCL and meniscal injury after knee trauma.
  • Fat embolism syndrome can produce respiratory dysfunction, neurological findings and petechial rash after trauma.
  • The lethal triad of major trauma is hypothermia, coagulopathy and acidosis.
01

The classic unhappy triad

  • A valgus force to a planted knee can injure the ACL, MCL and meniscus.
  • The historical description names the medial meniscus, while acute-injury series often identify lateral meniscal tears; imaging and examination establish the actual injury.
02

Fat embolism syndrome

  • Respiratory distress or hypoxemia, confusion or altered consciousness, and a petechial rash are classic clues.
  • Onset and severity vary; a normal initial examination does not eliminate subsequent concern after high-risk fracture.
03

Trauma lethal triad

  • Remember hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy as mutually reinforcing dangers in major bleeding.
  • Damage-control resuscitation seeks hemorrhage control, warming and correction of physiologic derangements.
04

Exam trap

  • Do not equate any knee injury with all components of the classic triad.
  • Persistent severe pain on passive stretch after trauma warrants consideration of compartment syndrome.
