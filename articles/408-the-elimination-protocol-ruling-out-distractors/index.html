The Elimination Protocol: Ruling Out Distractors

Absolute words, paired opposites, and outlier elimination rules...

Exam Strategy · MCQ Hacks

1. Beware of Absolutes:
Options containing absolute words such as 'always', 'never', 'only', 'exclusively' are almost always false in biological sciences. Options using qualifiers like 'most likely', 'often', 'frequently', 'may' are statistically more likely to be correct.

2. The 'Paired Opposites' Clue:
If two options are exact logical opposites (e.g., Option A: 'Increases heart rate' vs Option B: 'Decreases heart rate'), one of them is usually the correct answer, while the other two are distractors.

3. The 'True/False' Method for 'EXCEPT' Questions:
For questions containing 'EXCEPT' or 'NOT', write T or F next to each option. The lone 'F' option is your correct answer.
