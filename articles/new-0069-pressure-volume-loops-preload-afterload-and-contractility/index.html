Quick Revision

  • Increasing afterload tends to reduce stroke volume for a given contractility and preload.
  • Greater contractility increases the slope of end-systolic pressure-volume relation and lowers end-systolic volume.
01

Core biomedical mechanism

  • The left ventricular pressure-volume loop displays filling, isovolumetric contraction, ejection and isovolumetric relaxation.
  • Increasing preload generally increases end-diastolic volume and stroke volume within physiological limits.
02

Clinical application

  • Increasing afterload tends to reduce stroke volume for a given contractility and preload.
  • Greater contractility increases the slope of end-systolic pressure-volume relation and lowers end-systolic volume.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Changes are interdependent; an altered loop should be interpreted using the axis, valve events and clinical setting.
