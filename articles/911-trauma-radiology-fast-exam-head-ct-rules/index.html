Trauma Radiology: FAST Exam & Head CT Rules

FAST/eFAST provides rapid point-of-care ultrasound clues in trauma, but negative views do not exclude every important injury.

Quick Revision

  • FAST looks for free intraperitoneal or pericardial fluid in a trauma setting.
  • Extended FAST adds thoracic ultrasound observations to assess selected chest injuries including pneumothorax or hemothorax.
  • Performance depends on operator skill, timing, imaging windows and clinical context.
01

What FAST is designed to detect

  • Focused views assess the pericardial region and dependent abdominal spaces for free fluid.
  • An unstable trauma patient may require urgent action despite incomplete imaging.
02

What extended FAST adds

  • Assess lung sliding and other ultrasound signs in the correct anatomical context; no single sign is sufficient in every patient.
  • Use full trauma assessment and definitive imaging when indicated in stable patients.
03

Exam trap

  • A negative FAST does not exclude retroperitoneal injury or every intra-abdominal injury.
  • A normal lung sliding assessment is not a universal rule-out test for all chest emergencies.
