Tuning Fork Tests & Pure Tone Audiometry (PTA)

Rinne, Weber, ABC tests, Carhartundefineds notch, and Stengerundefineds malingering test...

Third Year (Part 1) · ENT

Tuning Fork Tests (512 Hz preferred to prevent tone decay/overtones):
• Rinne Test: Compares Air Conduction (AC) vs Bone Conduction (BC).
  - Positive (AC > BC): Normal ear or Sensorineural Hearing Loss (SNHL).
  - Negative (BC > AC): Conductive Hearing Loss (CHL) (Minimum 15–20 dB air-bone gap required).
  - False Negative Rinne: Severe unilateral dead ear / profound SNHL; sound heard via bone conduction by the contralateral normal ear (masking required).
• Weber Test: Lateralization of sound via skull midline bone conduction.
  - Lateralizes to Affected (Poorer) Ear: Conductive Hearing Loss (ambient noise attenuated in diseased ear).
  - Lateralizes to Better (Normal) Ear: Sensorineural Hearing Loss.
• Absolute Bone Conduction (ABC) Test: Reduced in SNHL; normal in CHL.

High-Yield Audiometric Patterns:
• Otosclerosis: Bilateral progressive CHL, Paracusis of Willis (hears better in noisy surroundings), Schwartz sign (flamingo-pink promontory), Carhart's Notch (dip in bone conduction curve at 2000 Hz / 2 kHz). Treatment: Stapedotomy with Teflon piston insertion.
• Noise-Induced Hearing Loss (Acoustic Trauma): Bilateral symmetrical SNHL with a distinct dip / acoustic notch at 4000 Hz (4 kHz).
• Presbyacusis: Age-related high-frequency symmetrical down-sloping SNHL.
