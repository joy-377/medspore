Immunobullous Diseases: Pemphigus vs Pemphigoid

Desmoglein vs BP antigens, Nikolsky sign, and DIF patterns...

Final Year (Part 2) · Dermatology

Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV):
• Pathogenesis: Autoantibodies against Desmoglein-3 (mucosal) and Desmoglein-1 (skin) disrupting intraepidermal keratinocyte adhesion.
• Blister Type: Flaccid, fragile bullae that rupture extremely easily leaving painful, non-healing erosions. Extensive oral mucosal involvement is the presenting hallmark.
• Clinical Signs: Nikolsky Sign Positive (slight lateral pressure on normal skin produces epidermal slippage); Asboe-Hansen (Bulla-spread) Sign Positive.
• Histopathology & DIF: Intraepidermal clefting directly suprabasal (Tombstone appearance of basal cells) with Acantholysis (Tzanck smear reveals round acantholytic cells). Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF) reveals a Fishnet / Reticular intercellular pattern of IgG and C3 throughout the epidermis.

Bullous Pemphigoid (BP):
• Pathogenesis: Autoantibodies against hemidesmosomal proteins BP180 (BPAG2) and BP230 (BPAG1) at the dermo-epidermal junction.
• Blister Type: Tense, thick-walled bullae arising on an urticarial, erythematous, severely pruritic base. Oral mucosa usually spared.
• Clinical Signs: Nikolsky Sign Negative; Bulla-spread sign negative.
• Histopathology & DIF: Subepidermal non-acantholytic split packed with eosinophils. DIF demonstrates a continuous Linear band of IgG and C3 along the dermo-epidermal basement membrane.
