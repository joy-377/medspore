Congenital Heart Diseases: Acyanotic vs Cyanotic

VSD, ASD, Tetralogy of Fallot, TGA, and classic chest radiograph silhouettes...

Final Year (Part 2) · Pediatrics

Acyanotic Congenital Heart Diseases (Left-to-Right Shunts):
• Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD): Most common congenital heart disease overall. Presents with a loud, harsh Holosystolic murmur at the left lower sternal border. Small defects (Maladie de Roger) produce louder murmurs than large defects.
• Atrial Septal Defect (ASD - Ostium Secundum most common): Wide, Fixed split S2; ejection systolic murmur at pulmonary area (due to increased flow, not the defect itself).
• Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA): Continuous 'machinery murmur' loudest at left infraclavicular area, bounding pulses with wide pulse pressure. Treated with IV Indomethacin / Ibuprofen in preterms.

Cyanotic Congenital Heart Diseases (Right-to-Left Shunts):
• Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF - Most common cyanotic heart disease after infancy):
  - Components: 1. VSD, 2. Pulmonary Stenosis (infundibular - determines severity), 3. Overriding of Aorta, 4. Right Ventricular Hypertrophy.
  - Chest X-Ray: 'Boot-shaped heart' (Coeur en sabot) with oligemic lung fields.
  - Hypercyanotic / 'Tet' Spells: Sudden infundibular spasm. Managed by Knee-chest position (increases SVR, decreases R-to-L shunt) + Oxygen + Morphine + IV Propranolol.
• Transposition of the Great Arteries (TGA - Most common cyanotic in neonates): Aorta arises from RV, Pulmonary artery from LV. CXR shows 'Egg-on-a-string' appearance. Life-saving emergency: Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil) infusion to keep PDA open until Rashkind balloon atrial septostomy.
