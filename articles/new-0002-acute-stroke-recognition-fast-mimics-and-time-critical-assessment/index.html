Quick Revision

  • A posterior-circulation stroke can present with gait ataxia, diplopia, dysarthria or severe imbalance even without classic FAST findings.
  • Record last-known-well time and assess urgent brain imaging to separate ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke.
01

Recognize the situation

  • FAST highlights Face weakness, Arm weakness and Speech disturbance; Time means activating emergency assessment without delay.
  • Check capillary glucose because hypoglycemia can mimic stroke and may be rapidly reversible.
02

Clinical decisions & differential

  • A posterior-circulation stroke can present with gait ataxia, diplopia, dysarthria or severe imbalance even without classic FAST findings.
  • Record last-known-well time and assess urgent brain imaging to separate ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke.
03

Memory aid

  • FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech disturbance, Time to activate stroke care.
  • FAST does not reliably cover all posterior circulation presentations: new ataxia, diplopia or severe imbalance still require urgent evaluation.
04

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • A normal initial noncontrast CT does not rule out early ischemic stroke.
