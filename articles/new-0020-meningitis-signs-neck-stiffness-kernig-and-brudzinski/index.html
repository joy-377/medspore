01

Recognize the pattern

  • Meningeal irritation may produce neck stiffness, photophobia and headache, but bedside signs are not sensitive enough to exclude meningitis.
  • Kernig and Brudzinski signs are classic examination findings; their absence does not reliably rule out disease.
02

What this finding does—and does not—mean

  • Altered consciousness, seizures, petechial rash or hemodynamic instability signal higher urgency.
  • Prompt clinical assessment and appropriate treatment matter; lumbar puncture decisions account for safety and contraindications.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Do not delay urgent antimicrobials in a severely unwell patient solely to obtain a textbook-perfect sign.
