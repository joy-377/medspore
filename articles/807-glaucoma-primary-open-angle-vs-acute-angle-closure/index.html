Glaucoma: Primary Open-Angle vs Acute Angle-Closure

Optic disc cupping, gonioscopy, IOP spikes, and laser iridotomy management...

Third Year (Part 1) · Ophthalmology

Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma (POAG):
• Presentation: Asymptomatic, insidious, painless bilateral visual field loss (tunnel vision). IOP often chronically elevated (>21 mmHg). Angle open on Gonioscopy (all 4 structures visible: Schwalbe's line, TM, Scleral spur, Ciliary body band).
• Optic Disc Changes: Vertical cup-to-disc ratio > 0.6, asymmetry between eyes > 0.2, bayoneting of vessels, disc hemorrhages.
• Visual Field Defects: Isopter contraction → Seidel scotoma → Bjerrum arcuate scotoma → Roenne nasal step → Tubular vision.
• First-Line Medical Therapy: Prostaglandin Analogues (Latanoprost, Bimatoprost - increases uveoscleral outflow).

Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma (AACG):
• Presentation: Sudden excruciating eye pain, headache, nausea/vomiting, colored halos around lights (corneal epithelial edema). Rapid vision drop. Precipitated by mydriasis (dark room, anticholinergics).
• Examination: Stony hard eyeball, IOP markedly elevated (50–70 mmHg), steamy/hazy cornea, shallow anterior chamber, mid-dilated, vertically oval, non-reactive pupil.
• Acute Medical Management: IV Mannitol (osmotic agent) + Topical Timolol + Topical Apraclonidine + Oral Acetazolamide. Pilocarpine (2%) instilled once IOP drops below 30 mmHg (relieves pupillary block).
• Definitive Management: Nd:YAG Laser Peripheral Iridotomy (LPI) in the affected eye AND prophylactic LPI in the contralateral fellow eye.
