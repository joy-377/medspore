Quick Revision

  • Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia can cause bilirubin neurotoxicity when severe; conjugated jaundice suggests other pathology.
  • Use age- and risk-specific nomograms or approved guidance for phototherapy decisions.
01

Clinical syndrome and red flags

  • Newborn jaundice requires interpretation by age in hours, gestation, bilirubin level and risk factors.
  • Jaundice within the first day of life is concerning and needs prompt assessment.
02

Differentiate and manage

  • Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia can cause bilirubin neurotoxicity when severe; conjugated jaundice suggests other pathology.
  • Use age- and risk-specific nomograms or approved guidance for phototherapy decisions.
03

Memory aid

  • Neonatal jaundice red flags: unusually early onset, fast progression, illness, poor feeding and prolonged jaundice.
  • Always interpret bilirubin against the infant’s age, gestation and risk factors rather than one universal threshold.
04

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Do not decide treatment from a bilirubin number without age in hours and gestation.
