Arterial Blood Gas (ABG): Stepwise Evaluation

Winterundefineds formula, Serum Anion Gap, and Delta Gap calculations explained...

Critical Lab Values · Hematology & ABG

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Step 1: Check pH (<7.35 = Acidemia, >7.45 = Alkalemia).
Step 2: Check Primary Driver (pCO2 vs HCO3). If pH and pCO2 move in opposite directions, it is Respiratory; if same direction, Metabolic.

Step 3: Anion Gap (for Metabolic Acidosis):
Serum AG = Na - (Cl + HCO3). Normal = 12 ± 2 mEq/L.
High AG Acidosis Causes (GOLDMARK): Glycols, Oxoproline, L-lactate, D-lactate, Methanol, Aspirin, Renal failure, Ketoacidosis.

Step 4: Compensation (Winter's Formula):
Expected pCO2 = (1.5 × HCO3) + 8 ± 2.
• Measured pCO2 > Expected = Concurrent Respiratory Acidosis.
• Measured pCO2 < Expected = Concurrent Respiratory Alkalosis.

Delta Ratio (ΔAG / ΔHCO3):
• <0.4: Pure Normal AG Metabolic Acidosis.
• 0.4 - 0.8: Mixed High AG and Normal AG Acidosis.
• 1 - 2: Pure High AG Acidosis (classic DKA).
• >2: High AG Acidosis + concurrent Metabolic Alkalosis.
