Classic Chest X-Ray (CXR) Signs

Recognize major chest radiograph patterns while remembering that classic X-ray signs are neither universally present nor diagnostic alone.

Quick Revision

  • A deep sulcus on a supine radiograph can suggest pneumothorax.
  • A Golden S sign can arise from lobar collapse associated with a central obstructing lesion.
  • Hampton hump and Westermark sign are historical clues to pulmonary embolism but are insensitive.
01

Pneumothorax and collapse

  • Deep sulcus: abnormally deep lateral costophrenic angle on supine chest imaging in some pneumothoraces.
  • Golden S sign: S-shaped displaced fissure associated with an upper-lobe collapse pattern and possible central mass.
02

Pulmonary vascular clues

  • Hampton hump: peripheral pleural-based wedge opacity can reflect pulmonary infarction.
  • Westermark sign: focal oligemia may follow pulmonary artery obstruction, but is rarely sufficient for diagnosis.
03

A large cardiac silhouette

  • A globular “water bottle” cardiac silhouette may accompany a large pericardial effusion.
  • Echocardiography assesses pericardial fluid and physiology when clinically indicated.
04

Exam trap

  • A normal chest radiograph does not exclude pulmonary embolism.
  • A silhouette sign may have several causes; interpret with the clinical picture and appropriate imaging.
