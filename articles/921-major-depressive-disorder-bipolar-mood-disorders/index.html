Major Depressive Disorder & Bipolar Mood Disorders

Separate major depressive episodes from mania/hypomania and remember the safety considerations of lithium therapy.

Quick Revision

  • Major depression combines a sustained cluster of mood, interest, energy, cognitive and vegetative symptoms with functional impact.
  • Bipolar I disorder requires a history of mania; screening for past mania is essential before treating depressive symptoms.
  • Lithium needs kidney, thyroid, interactions and concentration monitoring because toxicity risk changes with hydration and renal function.
01

Depression: the clinical syndrome

  • Look for persistent depressed mood or anhedonia alongside sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, guilt and suicide-related symptoms.
  • Evaluate medical, substance-related and medication contributors and assess safety.
02

Bipolar differential and medication safety

  • Mania includes a distinct period of elevated or irritable mood with increased energy, altered sleep and changes in judgment or behavior.
  • Lithium clearance is sensitive to renal function, dehydration and interacting drugs such as selected diuretics and NSAIDs.
03

Exam trap

  • A patient presenting with depression may have a past history of hypomania or mania, affecting treatment decisions.
  • Do not assume a single lithium therapeutic concentration range is suitable for every patient or laboratory.
