Differentiating the Types of Shock Clinically

Hypovolemic vs Cardiogenic vs Distributive vs Obstructive shock parameters...

Clinical Tips · General Tips

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Understanding the hemodynamic parameters of shock helps in rapid bedside diagnosis before central line placement.

  • Hypovolemic Shock (e.g., Trauma/Hemorrhage): Cold, clammy extremities. Vitals: Low BP, Tachycardia. Hemodynamics: Decreased Cardiac Output (CO), Decreased Preload (PCWP), Increased Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR) as a compensatory mechanism.
  • Cardiogenic Shock (e.g., Massive MI): Cold, clammy extremities with pulmonary crackles (fluid backup). Hemodynamics: Decreased CO, INCREASED Preload (PCWP), Increased SVR.
  • Distributive Shock (e.g., Sepsis/Anaphylaxis): Warm, flushed extremities initially (due to vasodilation). Hemodynamics: Increased CO (initially), Decreased Preload, DECREASED SVR (hallmark).
  • Obstructive Shock (e.g., Cardiac Tamponade, Massive PE): Muffled heart sounds or distended neck veins. Hemodynamics: Decreased CO, Increased PCWP, Increased SVR.
