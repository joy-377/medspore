Postoperative Fever: A Clinical Assessment Framework

Wind, Water, Walking, Wound, Wonder Drugs. The timeline of post-op fever...

Clinical Tips · Ward Protocols

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

The traditional “five Ws” are a memory aid, not a reliable diagnostic algorithm. Postoperative fever must be assessed using the patient’s vital signs, procedure, clinical examination, risk factors, and timeline.

Look for urgency first

  • Assess haemodynamic instability, respiratory distress, wound findings and sepsis risk.
  • Consider surgical site infection, pulmonary causes, urinary infection, thrombosis and medication-related fever when clinically appropriate.
  • Order tests according to clinical findings rather than postoperative day alone.
  • Important: Atelectasis by itself should not be assumed to cause postoperative fever.
