Antibiotic Stewardship: Empirical Choices

Empirical antimicrobial decisions balance immediate infection risk with local susceptibility and safe de-escalation.

Quick Revision

  • Start by identifying the likely site of infection, severity, host factors and likely pathogens.
  • Check allergy, renal or hepatic impairment, prior antibiotic exposure and local resistance data.
  • Reassess cultures and response promptly; narrow or stop unnecessary therapy when clinically appropriate.
01

Initial selection

  • A rapidly deteriorating patient may require urgent empiric treatment after appropriate cultures, without dangerous delay.
  • Drug choice varies by syndrome, local resistance, patient factors and infection-control setting.
02

Review and stewardship

  • Ensure source control when needed and check the adequacy of route, penetration and targeted coverage.
  • Review therapy when microbiology and the clinical course clarify the diagnosis; duration is syndrome-specific.
03

Exam trap

  • The newest or broadest antibiotic is not universally the best treatment.
  • An arbitrary fixed duration cannot replace source control, response and guideline context.
