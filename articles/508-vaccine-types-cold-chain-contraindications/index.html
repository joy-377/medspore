Vaccine Types, Cold Chain & Contraindications

Vaccine effectiveness depends on product-specific storage, correct handling and age-appropriate administration.

Quick Revision

  • Vaccines include live attenuated, inactivated, toxoid, recombinant and conjugate formulations.
  • Cold-chain requirements are specific to the product; freezing can permanently damage certain vaccines.
  • If a temperature excursion occurs, quarantine affected doses and follow program/manufacturer advice before use or disposal.
01

Recognize vaccine platforms

  • Live attenuated vaccines contain weakened organisms; inactivated and recombinant platforms do not contain replication-competent live pathogens.
  • Conjugation enhances immune responses to selected polysaccharide antigens, especially in young children.
02

Practical storage principles

  • Many refrigerator-stored vaccines require a controlled 2–8°C range, but product labeling and national guidance determine exact conditions.
  • Freeze-sensitive products should not be stored where accidental freezing can occur; temperature monitoring and documented handling are essential.
03

Exam trap

  • “Refrigerated” does not mean every vaccine has identical storage requirements.
  • Do not assume an accidental temperature excursion is harmless or automatically discard stock before contacting responsible authorities.
