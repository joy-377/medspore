Schizophrenia, Mood Disorders & Antipsychotic EPS

Schneiderian first-rank symptoms, Bipolar criteria, and managing acute dystonia...

Final Year (Part 2) · Psychiatry

Schneider's First-Rank Symptoms (FRS) of Schizophrenia:
• Auditory Hallucinations: 1. Voices discussing or arguing (third person), 2. Voices commenting on one's actions, 3. Thought echo (Gedankenlautwerden).
• Delusional Perception: A normal perception interpreted with a private, highly personalized, abnormal significance.
• Passivity Phenomena / Delusions of Control: Feeling that one's thoughts (insertion, withdrawal, broadcast), feelings, impulses, or somatic movements are controlled by an outside external agency.

Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS) of Antipsychotics:
• Acute Dystonia (Hours to Days): Involuntary, painful spastic contractions of muscle groups (Torticollis, Oculogyric crisis, Trismus). Treatment: IV Promethazine or Anticholinergics (Benztropine / Trihexyphenidyl).
• Akathisia (Days to Weeks): Subjective inner motor restlessness and compulsion to pace around. Treatment: Propranolol (Beta-blocker) is first-line; Benzodiazepines second-line.
• Pseudoparkinsonism (Weeks to Months): Cogwheel rigidity, resting pill-rolling tremor, masked facies, bradykinesia. Treatment: Oral Trihexyphenidyl / Benztropine.
• Tardive Dyskinesia (Months to Years): Repetitive, involuntary choreoathetoid movements of tongue, face, and lips (lip-smacking, tongue protrusion). Caused by dopamine receptor hypersensitivity. Treatment: VMAT-2 inhibitors (Valbenazine, Deutetrabenazine); switch to Clozapine.
