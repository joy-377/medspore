High-Yield Eponymous Nodes & Spots

Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, Roth spots, Koplik spots and named metastatic nodules are useful pattern-recognition clues.

Quick Revision

  • Painful Osler nodes and painless Janeway lesions are classically associated with infective endocarditis, but may be absent.
  • Koplik spots can appear with measles before the typical exanthem.
  • Virchow node and Sister Mary Joseph nodule can signal underlying malignancy and require proper diagnostic assessment.
01

Infective endocarditis signs

  • Osler nodes: classically tender nodules on distal digits.
  • Janeway lesions: classically painless lesions of palms or soles.
  • Roth spots: retinal hemorrhagic lesions with pale centers; they are not exclusive to endocarditis.
02

Viral illness clue

  • Koplik spots are small whitish buccal mucosal lesions classically associated with measles.
  • Recognize the importance of infection-control precautions and clinical confirmation when measles is suspected.
03

Named malignant associations

  • Virchow node: left supraclavicular lymphadenopathy may reflect thoracic or abdominal malignancy but has several possible causes.
  • Sister Mary Joseph nodule: an umbilical metastatic deposit can occur with intra-abdominal or pelvic malignancy.
04

Exam trap

  • Neither a missing skin sign nor a named node excludes the underlying disease.
  • Clinical signs should trigger targeted investigation rather than be treated as standalone diagnostic proof.
