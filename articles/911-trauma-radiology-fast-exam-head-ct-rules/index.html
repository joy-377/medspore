Trauma Radiology: FAST Exam & Head CT Rules

Focused Assessment with Sonography in Trauma (FAST) 4 standard acoustic windows...

Final Year (Part 2) · Radiology

The 4 Standard Acoustic Windows in FAST:
• 1. Perihepatic / Morison's Pouch (Hepatorenal Recess): Most sensitive dependent area for free intraperitoneal fluid in a supine patient. Probe placed in right mid-axillary line between 8th and 11th ribs.
• 2. Perisplenic Window (Splenorenal Recess): Examines potential space between spleen and left kidney. Probe placed in left posterior axillary line (intercostal space 9-11).
• 3. Pelvic Window (Retrovesical / Pouch of Douglas): Evaluates rectovesical pouch in males or rectouterine pouch in females. Probe placed suprapubically in longitudinal and transverse planes (bladder must ideally be full).
• 4. Pericardial Window (Subxiphoid View): Evaluates pericardial space for hemopericardium / tamponade. Probe directed angled toward left shoulder beneath the xiphoid process.

Extended FAST (eFAST): Adds bilateral anterior thoracic lung views to rapidly rule out Pneumothorax (absence of normal lung sliding and absence of 'comet-tail' artifacts; presence of a 'Barcode / Stratosphere Sign' on M-mode) and Hemothorax (fluid in costophrenic angles).
