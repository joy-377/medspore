Hepatobiliary & GI Triads

Classic gastrointestinal triads help recognize cholangitis, gastric volvulus and esophageal rupture without replacing diagnostic assessment.

Quick Revision

  • Charcot triad is fever, right-upper-quadrant pain and jaundice; the full triad is not always present in cholangitis.
  • Reynolds pentad adds hypotension and altered mentation to Charcot triad.
  • Borchardt triad and Mackler triad are important differential clues for acute gastric volvulus and esophageal rupture.
01

Charcot triad: acute cholangitis

  • Biliary obstruction with infection can cause upper abdominal pain, fever and jaundice.
  • Management involves urgent clinical assessment, antibiotics and timely biliary drainage when indicated.
02

Reynolds pentad: systemic compromise

  • Charcot triad plus hypotension and altered mental status raises concern for severe cholangitis.
  • Severe disease may occur without all five classic findings.
03

Borchardt and Mackler patterns

  • Borchardt: severe epigastric pain, retching with little or no vomit and difficulty passing a nasogastric tube suggest acute gastric volvulus.
  • Mackler: vomiting, chest pain and subcutaneous emphysema can occur after esophageal rupture, although the full combination is uncommon.
04

Exam trap

  • Classic triads are memory aids; the absence of a complete triad does not exclude a serious diagnosis.
