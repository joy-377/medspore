Core biomedical mechanism

  • Cranial nerve localization combines the pattern of motor, sensory and autonomic deficits rather than one isolated sign.
  • A lower motor neuron facial palsy often affects the forehead; typical unilateral supranuclear facial weakness tends to spare forehead movement.
02

Clinical application

  • Afferent pupillary light input travels via optic nerve II; efferent constriction travels via oculomotor nerve III.
  • Bulbar symptoms, diplopia and pupil abnormalities may signal brainstem or compressive disease requiring urgent assessment.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Forehead sparing is a helpful pattern but not an absolute rule in every neurological lesion.
