Clinical Virology: Hepatitis & Retroviruses

Hepatitis B serology interpretation and HIV replication kinetics...

Second Year MBBS · Microbiology

Hepatitis B Serology Master Interpretation:
• HBsAg: Indicates active infection (acute or chronic). If present >6 months = Chronic carrier/infection.
• Anti-HBs (HBsAb): Indicates immunity / cure (Present in vaccinated individuals and those recovered from natural infection).
• Anti-HBc IgM: Marker of Acute infection. Sole diagnostic marker present during the 'Window Period' (when HBsAg has cleared but Anti-HBs is not yet detectable).
• Anti-HBc IgG: Marker of past natural exposure (Positive in chronic infection and natural recovery; NEVER positive after vaccination).
• HBeAg / HBV DNA: Indicates active viral replication and high infectivity.

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) Diagnostics:
• Structural Genes: env (gp120 for CD4 attachment, gp41 for fusion); gag (p24 capsid protein, used in 4th-generation ELISA screening); pol (Reverse Transcriptase, Integrase, Protease).
• Confirmatory / Diagnostic Standard: 4th generation antigen/antibody combo assay followed by HIV-1/HIV-2 differentiation immunoassay and quantitative viral load (RT-PCR).
• Opportunistic Infection Prophylaxis:
  - CD4 < 200 /µL: TMP-SMX for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP).
  - CD4 < 100 /µL: TMP-SMX for Toxoplasma gondii.
  - CD4 < 50 /µL: Azithromycin historically for Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC).
