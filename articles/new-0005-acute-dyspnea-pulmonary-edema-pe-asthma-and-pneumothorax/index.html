Recognize the situation

  • Acute dyspnea is a symptom; look for immediate threats such as pneumothorax, pulmonary edema, severe asthma and pulmonary embolism.
  • Unilateral reduced breath sounds with shock raises concern for tension pneumothorax; diffuse crackles may favor pulmonary edema.
02

Clinical decisions & differential

  • Wheeze is not specific to asthma, and a quiet chest during severe airflow obstruction may be ominous.
  • Pulse oximetry, work of breathing, hemodynamics, ECG and targeted imaging direct urgency.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • A normal oxygen saturation does not independently exclude pulmonary embolism.
