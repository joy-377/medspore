Pleural & Ascitic Fluid: Light's Criteria & SAAG

Light’s criteria categorize pleural effusions; the serum–ascites albumin gradient helps assess portal-hypertension physiology.

Quick Revision

  • Light’s criteria classify a pleural effusion as exudative when one or more protein/LDH ratios or the pleural LDH criterion is met.
  • A higher serum–ascites albumin gradient supports portal hypertension; fluid protein and context further distinguish etiologies.
  • Diuretic-treated heart failure may create an apparent exudate by Light’s criteria, requiring careful interpretation.
01

Pleural fluid: exudate versus transudate

  • Light’s criteria: exudate if pleural/serum protein ratio is at least 0.5, pleural/serum LDH ratio at least 0.6, or pleural LDH at least two-thirds the serum LDH upper limit.
  • Use the clinical differential: heart failure and systemic hydrostatic changes commonly produce transudates; infection, malignancy and inflammatory disease commonly produce exudates.
02

Ascitic fluid: the SAAG approach

  • SAAG is serum albumin minus ascitic albumin measured near the time of paracentesis.
  • A SAAG of at least 1.1 g/dL supports portal hypertension; a lower gradient points toward other mechanisms, with clinical exceptions.
03

Exam trap

  • Do not label a diuretic-treated cardiac effusion as inflammatory from borderline Light’s criteria alone.
  • Never interpret a gradient without considering sample context, cause and clinical findings.
