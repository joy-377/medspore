STEMI Localization & Culprit Coronary Arteries

ECG lead vectors, reciprocal depressions, and RV infarction precautions...

Final Year (Part 2) · Medicine

Coronary Artery Territory Localization on 12-Lead ECG:
• Anterior / Anteroseptal Wall (V1–V4): Left Anterior Descending (LAD) artery ('Widow Maker'). High risk of cardiogenic shock and heart block.
• Lateral Wall (I, aVL, V5, V6): Left Circumflex (LCx) artery or diagonal branch of LAD. Reciprocal depressions seen in inferior leads.
• Inferior Wall (II, III, aVF): Right Coronary Artery (RCA ~85%) or LCx (15%). Reciprocal ST depression in lead aVL.
• Posterior Wall (V7–V9): RCA or LCx. Standard leads V1–V3 show horizontal ST depression, tall upright T waves, and prominent R waves (R/S ratio > 1).

⚠️ Critical Pearl (Right Ventricular Infarction):
Occurs in ~30–40% of inferior STEMIs (proximal RCA occlusion). Identified by ST elevation in right precordial leads (V3R, V4R ≥ 1 mm).
• Clinical Presentation: Hypotension + Clear lung fields (absence of rales) + Elevated JVP.
• Contraindicated Drugs: Nitroglycerin, Morphine, and Diuretics (they reduce RV preload and trigger fatal circulatory collapse).
• Management: Aggressive IV Fluid boluses (0.9% Normal Saline) to maintain right ventricular filling pressures.
