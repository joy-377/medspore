Glomerular Diseases: Nephritic vs Nephrotic Syndromes

Nephritic and nephrotic patterns overlap but emphasize different types of glomerular injury.

Quick Revision

  • Nephritic disease features inflammation with hematuria and often reduced filtration or raised blood pressure.
  • Nephrotic disease features heavy protein loss, edema and hypoalbuminemia, with variable lipid changes.
  • Kidney biopsy and additional tests are selected according to age, presentation and suspected cause.
01

Recognize the syndrome

  • Nephritic: dysmorphic erythrocytes or red-cell casts suggest glomerular inflammation.
  • Nephrotic: prominent proteinuria with hypoalbuminemia can cause generalized edema.
02

Clinical correlations

  • Complement, infection history, autoimmune findings and renal function guide the differential.
  • Some glomerular disorders show mixed nephritic and nephrotic findings rather than clean textbook separation.
03

Exam trap

  • Do not diagnose a single glomerular disease solely from the syndrome label.
  • Edema by itself does not establish nephrotic syndrome.
