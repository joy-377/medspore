Postpartum Hemorrhage: Recognition and Emergency Response

Rapidly identify excessive postpartum bleeding, resuscitate and address uterine tone, tissue, trauma and coagulation.

Quick Revision

  • The four Ts—Tone, Tissue, Trauma and Thrombin—provide a practical framework for causes of postpartum hemorrhage.
  • Uterine atony is common, but lacerations, retained tissue and coagulopathy need active assessment.
  • A coordinated response combines uterine measures, uterotonics, resuscitation and appropriate escalation.
01

Recognize maternal shock

  • Blood loss may be underestimated visually, and vital signs may lag; assess cumulative loss and perfusion.
  • Activate emergency support early when bleeding persists or maternal physiology worsens.
02

Act on the cause

  • Assess uterine tone and genital tract trauma while coordinating blood products and definitive interventions when necessary.
  • Current evidence-based PPH bundles and local protocols guide dose, route and sequencing.
03

Memory aid

  • The four Ts of postpartum hemorrhage: Tone, Tissue, Trauma and Thrombin.
  • A firm uterus makes alternative sources such as trauma, tissue and coagulation particularly important to assess.
04

Exam trap

  • A firm uterus with ongoing bleeding should prompt attention to trauma, retained tissue and coagulation causes.
  • Do not delay escalation while waiting for an arbitrary blood-loss number.
