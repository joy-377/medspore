Syncope: Cardiac vs Neurological vs Vasovagal

Syncope is a transient loss of consciousness related to cerebral hypoperfusion; triggers, prodrome, exertional symptoms and cardiac findings guide risk.

Quick Revision

  • Vasovagal syncope often has a recognizable trigger and prodrome, while exertional or supine syncope raises cardiac concern.
  • Orthostatic symptoms follow standing and may reflect medication effects, dehydration or autonomic dysfunction.
  • Seizure, hypoglycemia and other causes can mimic syncope; recovery history helps with differentiation.
01

Start with red flags

  • Evaluate chest pain, palpitations, known heart disease, family history of sudden death, exertional collapse and abnormal ECG.
  • Check vital signs and other emergency causes when history suggests instability.
02

Compare common mechanisms

  • Vasovagal: prolonged standing, pain or emotional triggers with nausea, sweating or warmth before collapse are common.
  • Cardiac: arrhythmia or structural disease may produce sudden or exertional collapse, but absence of a prodrome is not obligatory.
  • Orthostatic: symptoms often follow standing; assess hydration, medicines and autonomic causes.
03

Exam trap

  • Seizure is a transient-loss-of-consciousness mimic, not simply another mechanism of circulatory syncope.
  • A normal initial ECG cannot exclude every intermittent arrhythmia.
