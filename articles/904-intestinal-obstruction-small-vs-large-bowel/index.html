Intestinal Obstruction: Small vs Large Bowel

Bowel obstruction is evaluated with clinical stability, imaging and signs of strangulation rather than an exact symptom-duration formula.

Quick Revision

  • Small-bowel obstruction commonly follows adhesions or hernia; large-bowel obstruction has different causes including malignancy and volvulus.
  • Vomiting, pain, distension and obstipation vary with level, completeness and duration of obstruction.
  • Peritonitis, escalating continuous pain, fever or physiologic deterioration raise concern for bowel ischemia and require urgent surgical review.
01

Compare small and large bowel patterns

  • Small bowel: prominent vomiting and central dilated loops may occur, with valvulae conniventes crossing the lumen on some radiographs.
  • Large bowel: greater distension and peripheral haustral patterns may appear; CT often better defines level and cause.
02

Assess danger and treatment direction

  • Evaluate dehydration, electrolyte disturbances, hernia and the possibility of a closed-loop obstruction.
  • The decision for surgery versus appropriate observation depends on ischemia signs, cause, severity and imaging.
03

Exam trap

  • A plain radiograph does not reliably exclude strangulation or ischemia.
  • Do not assume every bowel obstruction immediately requires laparotomy, or that persistent worsening signs can safely wait.
