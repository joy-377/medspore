Post-Mortem Changes: Algor, Livor & Rigor Mortis

Postmortem change is shaped by environment, body position and other variables; no single sign can determine an exact time of death.

Quick Revision

  • Algor mortis is cooling after death and depends on temperature, clothing, body habitus and airflow.
  • Livor mortis reflects gravity-dependent pooling; rigor mortis reflects postmortem ATP depletion in muscle.
  • A reliable forensic interpretation combines scene evidence, several physical findings and the known history.
01

What the changes represent

  • Algor: the body generally equilibrates with its environment, at a variable rate.
  • Livor: dependent color change can indicate body position but is not a stand-alone clock.
  • Rigor: cross-bridge persistence develops with ATP depletion; onset and disappearance vary.
02

Context matters

  • Decomposition, temperature, environment and postmortem handling can modify classic patterns.
  • An apparent fixed timeline in a textbook is only a crude teaching approximation, not an individual forensic estimate.
03

Exam trap

  • A fixed degrees-per-hour cooling rule is not reliable for an unknown scene.
  • Do not assert that a single observation proves an exact time or manner of death.
