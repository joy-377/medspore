CBC and Anemia Indices: A Practical Classification

Combine hemoglobin, MCV, reticulocyte response and smear findings to classify anemia logically.

Quick Revision

  • Microcytosis commonly prompts assessment for iron deficiency, thalassemia and chronic inflammatory processes.
  • Macrocytosis can reflect B12/folate deficiency, liver disease, alcohol, hypothyroidism or reticulocytosis.
  • A reticulocyte response helps distinguish reduced red-cell production from blood loss or hemolysis.
01

Start with red-cell indices

  • Mean corpuscular volume describes average red-cell size; morphology and distribution may reveal mixed populations.
  • Ferritin is affected by inflammation, so its diagnostic interpretation is context-dependent.
02

Use production-versus-loss logic

  • A low reticulocyte response despite anemia suggests insufficient marrow compensation.
  • An increased response suggests ongoing destruction, bleeding or recovery from treatment, depending on clinical context.
03

Exam trap

  • A single Mentzer-index cutoff is not definitive for thalassemia trait or iron deficiency.
  • Normal MCV does not exclude a mixed microcytic and macrocytic disorder.
