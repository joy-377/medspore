Firearm Wounds & Mechanical Injuries

Contact vs close range gunshot wounds, abraded collars, and lacerations vs incised wounds...

Third Year (Part 1) · Forensic Medicine

Firearm Entry Wounds Classification:
• Contact Range: Muzzle imprint on skin, stellate/star-shaped tearing over bony prominences (e.g., skull due to expanding pocket of gases), cherry-red discoloration from subcutaneous carbon monoxide absorption.
• Close Range (<6 inches): Singeing of hairs, burning of skin (flame effect), carbon sooting/blackening (smoke deposit, can be wiped off with water).
• Intermediate Range (up to 2–3 feet): Tattooing / Stippling (unburnt/partially burnt gunpowder grains embedded into the dermis; cannot be washed off).
• Distant Range (>3 feet): Pure mechanical projectile effect. Circular hole surrounded by an Abraded collar / Contusion margin and a dirt/grease collar. No tattooing, burning, or sooting present.

Incised Wound vs Lacerated Wound Differentiation:
• Incised Wound: Sharp weapon; length > depth; clean-cut regular margins; no tissue bridging; hair cut sharply; no bruising of surrounding skin.
• Lacerated Wound: Blunt force; irregular/torn margins; Tissue bridging present (nerve fibers, fibrous strands, and vessels stretch across the wound base); hair crushed/bulb intact; surrounding abrasion/bruising present.
