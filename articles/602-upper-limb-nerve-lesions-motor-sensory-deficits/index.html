Upper Limb Nerve Lesions: Motor & Sensory Deficits

Radial, Median, and Ulnar nerve compression sites and deformities...

First Year MBBS · Anatomy

Radial Nerve (C5–T1):
• Axilla Lesion (Crutch palsy / Saturday night palsy): Loss of triceps + Wrist Drop + Sensory loss on posterior arm/forearm and first dorsal web space.
• Spiral Groove Lesion (Shaft humerus fracture): Triceps spared (nerve branch arises higher); Wrist Drop present.
• Posterior Interosseous Nerve (PIN) Entrapment: Normal wrist extension (ECRL intact), but finger extension lost (Finger drop); no sensory loss.

Median Nerve (C5–T1):
• High Lesion (Supracondylar fracture humerus): Loss of pronation, weak wrist flexion, Benediction hand (pointing index when attempting to make a fist).
• Low Lesion (Carpal Tunnel Syndrome / Wrist cut): Ape-thumb deformity (loss of abductor pollicis brevis & opponens), thenar atrophy. Sensory loss over lateral 3.5 digits.

Ulnar Nerve (C8, T1):
• Cubital Tunnel / Medial Epicondyle (High): Mild clawing (FPD loss lessens clawing — Ulnar Paradox).
• Wrist / Guyon's Canal (Low): Severe Claw Hand. Froment's Sign positive (tests Adductor Pollicis; patient flexes thumb IP joint using FPL via median nerve to hold paper).
