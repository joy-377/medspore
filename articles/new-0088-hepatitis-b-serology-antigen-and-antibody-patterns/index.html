01

Pathophysiology & identification

  • HBsAg generally indicates current hepatitis B infection; anti-HBs commonly indicates immunity.
  • Anti-HBc indicates exposure to natural infection and is not induced by routine recombinant hepatitis B vaccination.
02

Diagnostic and treatment connections

  • IgM anti-HBc supports recent acute infection in the appropriate context.
  • The interpretation of isolated anti-HBc requires clinical context and sometimes additional testing.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Anti-HBs alone does not establish whether immunity arose from infection or vaccination without anti-HBc and history.
