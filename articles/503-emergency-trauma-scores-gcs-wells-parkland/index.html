Emergency & Trauma Scores: GCS, Wells & Parkland

High-Yield Tables · Clinical Scoring Systems

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS - Range 3 to 15):
• Eye Opening (E4): 4-Spontaneous, 3-To voice, 2-To pain, 1-None.
• Verbal Response (V5): 5-Oriented, 4-Confused, 3-Inappropriate words, 2-Incomprehensible sounds, 1-None.
• Motor Response (M6): 6-Obeys commands, 5-Localizes pain, 4-Withdraws from pain, 3-Decorticate (flexion), 2-Decerebrate (extension), 1-None.
Rule: GCS ≤ 8 indicates intubation ('GCS less than 8, intubate').

Wells Criteria for Pulmonary Embolism:
• Clinical signs of DVT (+3), PE most likely (+3), HR > 100 (+1.5), Immobilization/surgery in past 4 weeks (+1.5), Prior DVT/PE (+1.5), Hemoptysis (+1), Malignancy (+1).
Score > 4: PE Likely → CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA).
Score ≤ 4: PE Unlikely → High-sensitivity D-dimer.

Parkland Formula (Burn Resuscitation):
Fluid Volume (first 24h) = 4 mL × Weight (kg) × % Total Body Surface Area (TBSA) burned.
• First 8 hours: Administer 50% of the calculated volume (starting from the time of burn).
• Next 16 hours: Administer the remaining 50%.
• Fluid of choice: Ringer's Lactate (RL).
