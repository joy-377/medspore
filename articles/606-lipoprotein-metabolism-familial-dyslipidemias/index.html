Lipoprotein Metabolism & Familial Dyslipidemias

Apo markers, Frederickson classification, and eruptive vs tendinous xanthomas...

First Year MBBS · Biochemistry

Apoprotein Markers & Functions:
• Apo B-48: Mediates chylomicron secretion from enterocytes into lymphatics.
• Apo B-100: Structural protein of VLDL, IDL, LDL; binds to LDL receptor (Apo B-48 and B-100 derived from same gene via RNA editing).
• Apo E: Mediates remnant uptake (chylomicron remnants & IDL) by liver LDLR/LRP.
• Apo C-II: Essential cofactor that activates Lipoprotein Lipase (LPL).
• Apo A-I: Structural protein of HDL; activates LCAT for cholesterol esterification.

Frederickson Familial Dyslipidemias (High-Yield Types):
• Type I (Familial Hyperchylomicronemia): LPL or Apo C-II deficiency. Massive increase in Chylomicrons & Triglycerides (>1000 mg/dL). Creamy supernatant layer; Eruptive xanthomas, acute recurrent pancreatitis. No increased risk of CAD.
• Type IIa (Familial Hypercholesterolemia): Absent/defective LDL receptors or Apo B-100. Severe elevation of LDL & total cholesterol. Accelerated atherosclerosis, Tendon xanthomas (Achilles tendon), corneal arcus.
• Type III (Familial Dysbetalipoproteinemia): Apo E2 homozygosity (defective remnant uptake). Elevated Chylomicrons & VLDL remnants. Palmar xanthomas and early coronary disease.
