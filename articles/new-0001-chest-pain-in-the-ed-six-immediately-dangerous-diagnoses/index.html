Recognize the situation

  • Sudden chest pain with instability requires immediate assessment for acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, tamponade and esophageal rupture.
  • History, vital signs and a 12-lead ECG guide early triage, but a single normal ECG cannot exclude acute coronary syndrome.
02

Clinical decisions & differential

  • Aortic dissection may feature abrupt maximal pain and pulse or blood pressure asymmetry; embolism may present with tachycardia, dyspnea and pleuritic pain.
  • Use pretest probability and focused examination to choose imaging; urgent threats take priority over labeling pain musculoskeletal.
03

Memory aid

  • For unstable chest pain, systematically consider six threats: acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, tamponade and esophageal rupture.
  • This is a red-flag checklist, not a diagnosis from symptoms alone.
04

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Do not discharge a high-risk patient solely because the first troponin or ECG is normal.
