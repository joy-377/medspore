Gram-Positive Cocci & Bacilli Identification Algorithm

A small set of laboratory principles distinguishes major gram-positive cocci and bacilli before species-level confirmation.

Quick Revision

  • Gram-positive cocci are first grouped by arrangement and catalase results.
  • Staphylococci are typically catalase positive; streptococci and enterococci are typically catalase negative.
  • Culture features and biochemical tests are interpreted together, with species confirmation as needed.
01

Catalase and arrangement

  • Clusters of gram-positive cocci suggest staphylococci; chains or pairs suggest streptococci or enterococci, with exceptions.
  • Catalase testing can support the initial separation, but technique and specimen quality matter.
02

Coagulase and additional identification

  • Coagulase positivity is associated with Staphylococcus aureus; other staphylococci require appropriate species-level assessment.
  • Hemolysis patterns and targeted confirmatory tests support streptococcal and enterococcal differentiation.
03

Exam trap

  • Neither microscopic shape nor one bench test is sufficient for all clinical decisions.
  • Clinical relevance depends on the specimen source, contamination risk, patient status and susceptibility.
