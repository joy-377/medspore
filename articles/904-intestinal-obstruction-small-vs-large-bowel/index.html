Intestinal Obstruction: Small vs Large Bowel

Adhesions, Malignancy, Volvulus, and air-fluid level patterns on erect film...

Final Year (Part 2) · Surgery

Small Bowel Obstruction (SBO) vs Large Bowel Obstruction (LBO):
• Etiology: SBO is most commonly caused by Post-operative Adhesions (~60%) followed by Hernias; LBO is most commonly caused by Colorectal Malignancy followed by Diverticulitis and Volvulus.
• Pain Pattern: SBO presents with early, prominent, frequent vomiting and rapid colicky abdominal pain with modest distension; LBO presents with late vomiting, marked abdominal distension, and obstipation.

Abdominal Radiography (Erect X-Ray) Features:
• Small Bowel: Centrally located loops with Valvulae Conniventes (Plicae Circulares) extending completely across the entire width of the bowel lumen; multiple short air-fluid levels ('step-ladder pattern').
• Large Bowel: Peripherally located loops with Haustrations that do NOT cross the entire lumen width; frame-like distribution.

Strangulation Red Flags (Surgical Emergency): Continuous unremitting pain, fever, tachycardia, localized peritonitis, leukocytosis, and metabolic acidosis (elevated serum lactate). Warrants immediate exploratory laparotomy/laparoscopy.
