  • Reassess circulation, fluid balance, recent blood loss, renal function and nephrotoxic exposure.
  • Fluid challenges require evaluation for fluid responsiveness and overload; oliguria does not automatically mean dehydration.
01

Recognize the situation

  • Postoperative oliguria can follow hypovolemia, bleeding, sepsis, low cardiac output, obstruction or a medication effect.
  • Check catheter patency and whether the collected urine volume is real before interpreting low output.
02

Clinical decisions & differential

  • Reassess circulation, fluid balance, recent blood loss, renal function and nephrotoxic exposure.
  • Fluid challenges require evaluation for fluid responsiveness and overload; oliguria does not automatically mean dehydration.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • A blocked urinary catheter can mimic a fall in kidney urine production.
