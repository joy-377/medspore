Firearm Wounds & Mechanical Injuries

In forensic medicine, wound interpretation requires morphology, scene evidence and medical correlation rather than a single absolute sign.

Quick Revision

  • An incised wound usually has sharply defined edges and little tissue bridging.
  • Lacerations from blunt force often have irregular margins and tissue bridges, although overlap occurs.
  • Entry/exit interpretation in firearm injury requires scene, trajectory, range and weapon information.
01

Incised wound versus laceration

  • Sharp-force incisions often have relatively clean margins, whereas blunt-force lacerations can show crushing and bridges of tissue.
  • Context can complicate appearance, especially over bony prominences.
02

Interpret firearm injuries cautiously

  • Consider abrasion collars, soot, stippling, range, trajectory and secondary impacts only as a pattern of evidence.
  • A suspected entry or exit wound cannot be assigned accurately from a single surface feature in all cases.
03

Exam trap

  • Do not declare a definitive range or manner of injury from one isolated sign.
  • A thorough medico-legal examination requires documentation and appropriate chain of custody.
