Rapid Management of Hyperkalemia

ECG changes and the stepwise protocol to stabilize, shift, and excrete Potassium...

Clinical Tips · Ward Protocols

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Hyperkalemia (K+ > 5.5 mEq/L) is a medical emergency that can lead to fatal arrhythmias. ECG Changes: Tall peaked T waves → Loss of P wave → Widened QRS → Sine wave pattern → Asystole.

Step 1: Stabilize the Myocardium (Most Urgent)
Administer IV Calcium Gluconate (or Calcium Chloride via central line). Note: Calcium does NOT lower serum potassium; it only protects the heart by raising the threshold potential.

Step 2: Shift Potassium Intracellularly
• IV Insulin (Regular) + 50% Dextrose (to prevent hypoglycemia).
• Salbutamol (Albuterol) nebulization (Beta-2 agonist).
• IV Sodium Bicarbonate (especially if the patient is acidotic).

Step 3: Excrete Potassium from the Body
• Loop diuretics (Furosemide).
• GI cation-exchange resins (Patiromer, Sodium Zirconium Cyclosilicate).
• Hemodialysis (the ultimate definitive treatment if refractory).
