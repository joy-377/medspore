Neonatal Resuscitation: Ventilation Comes First

At birth, evaluate breathing and heart rate while preventing heat loss; effective ventilation is central when resuscitation is needed.

Quick Revision

  • Most newborns transition without resuscitation; assess breathing, tone and heart rate in an appropriate neonatal setting.
  • When assisted ventilation is needed, effectiveness is indicated by a rising heart rate.
  • If the heart rate fails to improve, reassess the airway and ventilation before escalating to compressions and other measures.
01

Prepare and assess

  • Anticipate risk, assemble the team and equipment, support thermoregulation and use appropriate cord management.
  • Use pulse oximetry to guide oxygen titration when needed.
02

Correct ventilation first

  • Mask seal, airway positioning and effective lung inflation are prerequisites to judging the response.
  • If bradycardia persists despite optimized ventilation, escalate via the current neonatal life-support algorithm.
03

Exam trap

  • Ineffective ventilation is a common correctable cause of persistent neonatal bradycardia.
  • Chest compressions are not the first response for every noncrying newborn.
