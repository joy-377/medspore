01

Pathophysiology & identification

  • Anticholinergic toxicity may cause dry skin, mydriasis, tachycardia, urinary retention, hyperthermia and delirium.
  • Cholinergic toxicity more often produces excess secretions, lacrimation, bronchorrhea and diarrhea.
02

Diagnostic and treatment connections

  • Supportive care, temperature control, ECG assessment and toxicology advice guide management.
  • Physostigmine has selected indications and contraindications; it is not a universal antidote for all unknown poisonings.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Dry versus wet secretions can be a useful discriminator, but mixed ingestions are common.
