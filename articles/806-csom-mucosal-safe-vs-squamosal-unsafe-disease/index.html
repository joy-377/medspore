CSOM: Mucosal (Safe) vs Squamosal (Unsafe) Disease

Atticoantral disease, Cholesteatoma, complications, and surgical strategies...

Third Year (Part 1) · ENT

Mucosal (Tubotympanic / 'Safe') CSOM:
• Perforation: Central (involves pars tensa; annulus tympanicus intact, does not touch bone margin).
• Discharge: Profuse, mucoid/mucopurulent, non-foul smelling, intermittent (associated with URTI or water entry).
• Cholesteatoma: Absent. Low risk of intracranial spread.
• Surgical Management: Tympanoplasty (underlay/overlay graft using temporalis fascia) after achieving a dry ear.

Squamosal (Atticoantral / 'Unsafe') CSOM:
• Perforation: Attic or marginal perforation (involves pars flaccida or reaches the bony sulcus tympanicus).
• Discharge: Scanty, purulent, serosanguinous, foul-smelling (bone erosion present).
• Cholesteatoma: Hallmark finding (keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium in middle ear cleft eroding ossicles via osteolytic collagenases).
• Life-Threatening Complications: Facial nerve palsy, labyrinthitis, acute mastoiditis, lateral sinus thrombophlebitis, meningitis, Temporal lobe / Cerebellar abscess.
• Surgical Management: Urgent Modified Radical Mastoidectomy (MRM) or Canal Wall Down procedure to eradicate disease.
