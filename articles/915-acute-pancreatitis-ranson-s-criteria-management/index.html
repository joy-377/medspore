Acute Pancreatitis: Ransonundefineds Criteria & Management

Gallstones vs Alcohol etiology, Cullen/Grey Turner signs, and fluid resuscitation...

Final Year (Part 2) · Surgery

Etiology & Clinical Signs:
• Top 2 Causes: Gallstones (~40-50%) and Chronic Alcohol abuse (~30%).
• Cullen's Sign: Superficial edema and bruising around the umbilicus (hemorrhagic pancreatitis).
• Grey Turner's Sign: Bruising of the flanks (retroperitoneal hemorrhage).

Ranson's Criteria for Severity Prediction:
• At Admission: GLUCOSE > 200 mg/dL, AGE > 55 years, LDH > 350 IU/L, HAST (AST) > 250 IU/L, WBC > 16,000 /µL.
• At 48 Hours: HCT drop > 10%, BUN rise > 5 mg/dL, Serum Calcium < 8 mg/dL, PaO2 < 60 mmHg, Base deficit > 4 mEq/L, Fluid sequestration > 6 L.
Scoring: Score 0–2 = Mild (<1% mortality); Score ≥ 3 = Severe pancreatitis warranting ICU monitoring.

Management: Aggressive isotonic crystalloid fluid resuscitation (Ringer's Lactate), pain control, and early enteral nutrition (oral feeding as soon as nausea/ileus resolves).
