01

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR)

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR):
• Non-Proliferative (NPDR): Earliest sign is Microaneurysms (outpouching of capillary wall in inner nuclear layer). Dot-and-blot hemorrhages, hard exudates (lipid deposits). Severe NPDR defined by the 4-2-1 Rule (>20 intraretinal hemorrhages in all 4 quadrants, venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, IRMA in ≥1 quadrant).
• Proliferative (PDR): Hallmark is Neovascularization (NVD at optic disc or NVE elsewhere) driven by VEGF from retinal ischemia. High risk of Vitreous Hemorrhage and Tractional Retinal Detachment. Treatment: Panretinal Photocoagulation (PRP) and anti-VEGF injections (Ranibizumab, Aflibercept).
• Diabetic Macular Edema (DME): Leading cause of moderate visual loss in diabetics; treatable with focal/grid laser or intravitreal anti-VEGF.

02

Hypertensive Retinopathy (Keith-Wagener-Barker Classification)

Hypertensive Retinopathy (Keith-Wagener-Barker Classification):
• Grade 1: Arteriolar attenuation, silver/copper wiring.
• Grade 2: Grade 1 + AV crossing changes (Salus sign, Gunn sign, Bonnet sign).
• Grade 3: Grade 2 + Flame hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots (microinfarcts of nerve fiber layer), hard exudates forming a Macular Star.
• Grade 4: Grade 3 + Papilledema (bilateral optic disc swelling; marks malignant hypertension crisis).

03

Rhegmatogenous Retinal Detachment (RRD)

Rhegmatogenous Retinal Detachment (RRD):
• Separation of neurosensory retina from retinal pigment epithelium due to a full-thickness retinal break/tear. Symptoms: Photopsia (flashes of light), floaters, and a progressive dark curtain falling over the field of vision. Ophthalmoscopy shows grey, elevated, corrugated retina undulating with eye movements.
