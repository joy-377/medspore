Cell Injury & Patterns of Tissue Necrosis

Coagulative, Liquefactive, Caseous, and Fibrinoid necrosis hallmarks...

Second Year MBBS · Pathology

Patterns of Tissue Necrosis:
• Coagulative Necrosis: Most common pattern. Architecture of dead tissues preserved for days (ghost cells). Characteristic of ischemic infarcts in all solid organs EXCEPT the brain.
• Liquefactive Necrosis: Complete digestion of dead cells transforming tissue into a liquid viscous mass. Seen in Hypoxic death of CNS / Brain infarcts and focal bacterial or fungal infections (pus formation / abscess).
• Caseous Necrosis: Friable, cheese-like macroscopic appearance. Microscopically shows structureless, granular, eosinophilic debris surrounded by granulomatous inflammation (giant cells, epithelioid cells). Classic for Tuberculosis.
• Fat Necrosis: Focal areas of fat destruction resulting from release of activated pancreatic lipases (Acute Pancreatitis) or breast trauma. Fatty acids combine with calcium to produce chalky-white deposits (Saponification).
• Fibrinoid Necrosis: Immune complex (antigen-antibody) deposition combined with fibrin leaking into arterial walls. Classic for Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN), Malignant Hypertension, and Aschoff nodules of Rheumatic Heart Disease.
