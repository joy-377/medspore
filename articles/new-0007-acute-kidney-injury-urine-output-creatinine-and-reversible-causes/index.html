01

Recognize the situation

  • Acute kidney injury is a change in kidney function detected by creatinine trend or reduced urine output, interpreted with timing and context.
  • Evaluate volume status, shock, nephrotoxins, obstruction and underlying kidney disease; review medication doses.
02

Clinical decisions & differential

  • A normal creatinine early in injury may be misleading because serum creatinine can lag behind a fall in GFR.
  • Urinalysis, electrolytes and appropriate renal imaging help distinguish functional, intrinsic and obstructive causes.
03

Exam trap — avoid this mistake

  • Do not assume all oliguria needs fluids; pulmonary edema and obstructive causes require different approaches.
