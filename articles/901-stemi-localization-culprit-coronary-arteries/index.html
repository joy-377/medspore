STEMI Localization & Culprit Coronary Arteries

Localize possible culprit coronary territories on the ECG without treating lead patterns as absolute arterial proof.

Quick Revision

  • Anterior ST elevation often implicates LAD territory; inferior ST elevation may reflect RCA or circumflex involvement.
  • Posterior infarction may produce reciprocal anterior ST depression; posterior leads can improve detection.
  • Right ventricular involvement changes hemodynamic management and requires careful assessment.
01

ECG territory clues

  • V1–V4 can suggest anterior involvement; II, III and aVF suggest inferior involvement; I, aVL, V5–V6 may suggest lateral involvement.
  • Coronary dominance and anatomical variation mean an ECG territory does not uniquely identify the occluded artery.
02

Assess right ventricular involvement

  • Inferior infarction with hypotension, clear lungs and raised jugular venous pressure should prompt right-sided ECG assessment and urgent specialist management.
  • Avoid therapies that could worsen hemodynamic instability; evaluate volume and right-heart physiology rather than using automatic aggressive fluid boluses.
03

Exam trap

  • A normal or nondiagnostic initial ECG does not fully exclude acute coronary syndrome.
  • Do not apply a single rigid medication rule without the patient’s blood pressure and clinical context.
