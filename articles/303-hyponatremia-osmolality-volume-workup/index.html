Hyponatremia: Osmolality & Volume Workup

Differentiating Hypovolemic, Euvolemic (SIADH), and Hypervolemic hyponatremia...

Critical Lab Values · Electrolytes

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Step 1: Confirm True Hyponatremia (Serum Osmolality):
• High Osmolality (>295 mOsm/kg): Hyperglycemia (Corrected Na = Measured Na + 1.6 × [(Glucose - 100) / 100]).
• Normal Osmolality (280–295): Pseudohyponatremia (severe hyperlipidemia or hyperproteinemia).
• Low Osmolality (<280): True Hypoosmolar Hyponatremia.

Step 2: Assess Volume Status:
• Hypovolemic: Dehydration, burns, diuretics. Urine Na < 20 = Extra-renal loss; Urine Na > 20 = Renal loss.
• Euvolemic: SIADH (Urine Osm > 100, Urine Na > 40), Hypothyroidism, Adrenal Insufficiency.
• Hypervolemic: Congestive Heart Failure, Cirrhosis, Nephrotic syndrome.

⚠️ Critical Pitfall (Correction Limit): Do not correct chronic hyponatremia faster than 8–10 mEq/L in 24 hours to avoid fatal Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (Central Pontine Myelinolysis).
