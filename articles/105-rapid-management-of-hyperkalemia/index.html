Hyperkalemia Emergencies: ECG Clues, Calcium and Potassium Removal

Learn why ECG findings matter, why calcium stabilizes the myocardium but does not remove potassium, and how to avoid missing pseudohyperkalemia.

Quick Revision

  • Intravenous calcium protects cardiac conduction but does not lower serum potassium.
  • Insulin-glucose causes a temporary intracellular shift; definitive removal is still needed.
  • Severity and ECG findings are not perfectly correlated.
  • Consider sample hemolysis if the laboratory result is discordant.
01

Recognize the risk

  • Hyperkalemia is an elevated blood potassium concentration; severity depends on the level, rate of rise, symptoms and ECG findings.
  • Significant hyperkalemia may cause conduction abnormalities, malignant arrhythmias and cardiac arrest.
  • A normal or minimally abnormal ECG does not reliably rule out dangerous hyperkalemia.
02

ECG patterns to recognize

  • Peaked T waves, prolonged PR interval, reduced P-wave amplitude, QRS widening and a sine-wave pattern are classic findings.
  • The ECG sequence is not obligatory; patients may lack classic changes despite significant hyperkalemia.
03

Emergency logic: protect → shift → remove

  • Protect the myocardium with intravenous calcium when indicated by significant ECG abnormalities or the emergency pathway. Calcium does not meaningfully lower serum potassium.
  • Temporarily shift potassium into cells using therapies such as insulin with glucose and, in selected cases, beta-2 agonists; monitor blood glucose.
  • Remove potassium from the body and address the cause; renal replacement therapy may be required in severe or refractory cases.
04

Exam trap: calcium is not potassium elimination

  • Calcium stabilizes cardiac excitability; insulin-glucose shifts potassium intracellularly; elimination requires kidney excretion, gastrointestinal removal or dialysis.
  • Do not delay urgent assessment while waiting for a textbook-perfect ECG pattern.
05

Memory aid

  • Emergency hyperkalemia: protect the heart → shift potassium → remove potassium.
  • Calcium protects cardiac electrical stability but does not remove potassium.
06

Pseudohyperkalemia and follow-up

  • Consider a hemolyzed sample or collection artifact when the result does not fit the clinical context.
  • Repeat the sample promptly when appropriate, while treating high-risk symptomatic or ECG-positive patients urgently.
  • Recheck potassium and glucose after therapy and monitor for rebound.
