Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy & Eclampsia

Preeclampsia diagnostic criteria, HELLP syndrome, and MgSO4 Pritchard regimen...

Final Year (Part 2) · OBG

Preeclampsia Diagnostic Criteria (After 20 Weeks Gestation):
• New-onset Hypertension (SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg on two occasions 4 hours apart) in a previously normotensive woman, WITH Proteinuria (≥ 300 mg/24h or urine protein/Cr ratio ≥ 0.3).
• In the absence of proteinuria: Diagnosed if hypertension occurs alongside thrombocytopenia (<100k), renal insufficiency (Cr > 1.1), impaired liver function (transaminases 2x normal), pulmonary edema, or persistent new cerebral/visual disturbances.

HELLP Syndrome: Hemolysis (schistocytes, elevated LDH >600), Elevated Liver enzymes (AST/ALT ≥ 70), and Low Platelets (<100,000 /µL).

Eclampsia & Seizure Prophylaxis (Magnesium Sulfate - MgSO4):
• Pritchard Regimen:
  - Loading Dose: 4 g IV (20% solution over 5 min) PLUS 10 g IM (5 g of 50% solution deep IM in each buttock).
  - Maintenance Dose: 5 g IM (50% solution) every 4 hours in alternating buttocks, continuing until 24 hours postpartum or 24 hours after the last seizure.
• Monitoring Parameters for MgSO4 Toxicity: 1. Patellar reflex present (disappears first at 8–10 mEq/L), 2. Respiratory rate ≥ 16/min (respiratory arrest at 12–15 mEq/L), 3. Urine output ≥ 30 mL/hr (excreted solely by kidneys).
• Antidote for Mg Toxicity: 10 mL of 10% Calcium Gluconate IV administered slowly over 10 minutes.
