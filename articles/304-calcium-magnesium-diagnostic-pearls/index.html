Calcium & Magnesium Diagnostic Pearls

Corrected Calcium formula, ECG correlations, and refractory hypokalemia...

Critical Lab Values · Electrolytes

Learning note: Educational revision content; verify medication doses, protocols and reference ranges against current institutional guidelines before any patient-care use.

Corrected Calcium Formula:
Corrected Ca (mg/dL) = Measured Total Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 - Serum Albumin). Always calculate this in hypoalbuminemia before treating.

Hypocalcemia (Ca < 8.5 mg/dL):
• Signs: Chvostek's sign (facial twitch on tapping CN VII), Trousseau's sign (carpal spasm with BP cuff).
• ECG: Prolonged QT interval (high risk for Torsades de Pointes). Treatment: IV 10% Calcium Gluconate.

Hypercalcemia (Ca > 10.5 mg/dL):
• Etiologies: Primary Hyperparathyroidism (outpatient) vs Malignancy/PTHrP (inpatient).
• ECG: Shortened QT interval. Management: Vigorous IV Saline hydration + IV Bisphosphonates (Zoledronic acid) or Calcitonin.

The Magnesium Connection: Hypomagnesemia impairs PTH release and induces renal potassium wasting. If hypokalemia or hypocalcemia is refractory to treatment, always correct serum magnesium first.
