Lipoprotein Metabolism & Familial Dyslipidemias

Lipoprotein metabolism is easier when each apoprotein is linked to its key transport role.

Quick Revision

  • ApoB-48 is associated with intestinal chylomicrons; ApoB-100 supports LDL receptor binding.
  • ApoC-II supports lipoprotein lipase, while ApoE helps remnant clearance.
  • Very high triglycerides can raise pancreatitis risk, whereas LDL-associated disorders chiefly affect atherosclerotic risk.
01

Key apoproteins at a glance

  • ApoB-48: chylomicron structure and intestinal lipoprotein assembly.
  • ApoB-100: hepatic VLDL/LDL structural protein and LDL receptor interaction.
  • ApoC-II: activator of lipoprotein lipase; ApoE: hepatic remnant uptake; ApoA-I: HDL structure and LCAT activation.
02

Different genetic patterns

  • Familial chylomicronemia impairs clearance of triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and can cause recurrent pancreatitis.
  • Familial hypercholesterolemia commonly involves LDL receptor pathway defects with accelerated atherosclerosis and tendon xanthomas.
03

Exam trap

  • Severe triglyceride elevation is not interchangeable with isolated LDL elevation.
  • A normal total cholesterol result does not exclude every inherited lipoprotein disorder.
