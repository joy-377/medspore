Renal Function: Serum Creatinine, eGFR & BUN/Cr Ratio

Renal function tests require trend, baseline kidney disease and hemodynamic context; no single urine index proves the cause of AKI.

Quick Revision

  • Acute kidney injury may develop before creatinine rises noticeably; urine output and changes from baseline also matter.
  • Fractional excretion indices can support the assessment of sodium handling but may be misleading with diuretics, chronic kidney disease and sepsis.
  • Evaluate perfusion, nephrotoxins, obstruction and urine sediment alongside laboratory results.
01

Know the limitations of creatinine and urea

  • Serum creatinine reflects production and renal handling; muscle mass and illness can alter its interpretation.
  • Urea is influenced by protein turnover, volume depletion and gastrointestinal bleeding and should not be used as a stand-alone diagnostic discriminator.
02

Structured AKI assessment

  • Compare serial kidney function, fluid balance and urine output; review medications and exposure to contrast or toxins.
  • Assess volume status, examine urine sediment when indicated and use ultrasound if obstruction is suspected.
03

Exam trap: FENa is not a verdict

  • Urine sodium and fractional excretion may be altered by diuretics or mixed renal pathology.
  • A low FENa does not invariably exclude intrinsic renal disease, and a high FENa does not always exclude a prerenal component.
